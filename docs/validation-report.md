# Kill the Auction, Shrink the Map

The idea is viable only after two surgeries: remove "cheapest bid wins" and cut India and "worldwide" from the plan for at least three years. The underlying need is real. Nepali wedding and event photographers live off roughly **41 auspicious dates a year**, turn away work on those dates and sit idle in between, while walk-in studios are being hollowed out by smartphones. But the research turns up **no photographer marketplace anywhere that survived on open bidding**: the best-funded ones (Meero, Snappr, Kodakit) all set prices themselves and still struggled, TaskRabbit abandoned bidding in 2014 because jobs stopped completing, and Urban Company dropped photography outright in a market a hundred times Nepal's size. Nepal's own supply side already complains that newcomers undercut on price; a cheapest-wins platform would formalise the dynamic that established studios resent and would select for exactly the shooters a once-in-a-lifetime wedding client cannot afford to risk. The regulatory picture splits cleanly: a Nepal launch is a four-to-eight-week paperwork exercise, but a Nepal-funded Indian subsidiary runs into Nepal's 1964 ban on investing abroad (now a USD 20,000 cap), India's Press Note 3 approval regime, and the absence of any business-grade rail to pay Indian freelancers; those are walls, not hurdles. Nobody has published the numbers that would prove or disprove demand, so this report ranks the assumptions most likely to kill the business and names experiments that cost less than one wedding package each. The recommendation is to build a curated, deposit-backed booking service anchored on the family studio in one city cluster, monetised by date-lock deposits and vendor subscriptions rather than a race to the bottom, and to treat foreign demand for Nepali editing labour, not a global two-sided marketplace, as the only realistic export story.

## Nepal's studios suffer from 40 wedding dates, not from being undiscovered

The founder's diagnosis ("small local studios only get seasonal/event work") is correct but incomplete, because it merges two different businesses that are dying and thriving for different reasons. The first is the ward-level walk-in studio that lives on passport, ID and portrait prints. Independent reporting describes **"a steady decline in business across Nepal"** for these studios, with operators blaming smartphones and digitised paperwork and owners in eastern Nepal exiting over "unsustainable income levels" ([Rising Nepal](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/67505)); Kathmandu's iconic Photo Concern shrank from over **180 staff to 40** and was described in 2018 as the only legacy studio that remains ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/miscellaneous/2018/11/24/still-in-focus)). The baseload still exists, with **1,356,887 passports issued in FY 2024/25** ([myRepublica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/dop-issues-over-3600-e-passports-daily-63-90.html)), but free tools now generate compliant 35x45mm passport photos from a selfie ([Photogov](https://photogov.net/documents/np-passport-35x45-photo/)). A job-bidding marketplace does nothing for this problem; if the family studio is this kind of studio, the marketplace is a different business from the one it would rescue.

The second business is wedding, event and content shooting, and its problem is concentration, not absence, of demand. Only 11 of 27 nakshatras are suitable for marriage, so dates bunch tightly ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/another-wedding-season-in-nepal-why-too-few-dates-for-too-many-couples-every-year.html)); the BS 2083 almanac has **41 auspicious marriage dates, 12 of them in Falgun**, the busiest month by a wide margin ([Mero Kalam](https://merokalam.com/blog/nepali-calendar-marriage-dates/)). On such a date Kathmandu sees **"around 60 to 70 weddings"** and a photographer "usually cover[s] about six of them" ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)). Studios advise couples to book three to six months ahead in peak months while a few weeks suffices off-season ([Wedding Story Nepal](https://weddingstory.com.np/best-wedding-photographer-nepal)), and one studio owner describes the rhythm plainly: "We do have seasons and off-seasons, but we use the off-season for editing videos and photos" ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)). So the market gap, stated precisely, is twofold: on 40 or so dates there is excess demand that top studios cannot absorb and must turn away, and in Poush, the monsoon and Chaturmas there is near-zero wedding demand that needs filling with something else. That is a scheduling and overflow problem, which is a very different product from a general-purpose bidding board.

Pricing is dispersed enough that clients genuinely cannot compare, which is the one thing that favours a marketplace. The table below assembles vendor list prices from the notes; they are marketing figures, not a realised-price survey.

A twenty-fold spread for nominally the same "wedding package" means clients buy on trust and portfolio, not on a price list. It also means a cheapest-wins mechanism would compress bids toward the NPR 15,000–25,000 floor and drive the NPR 75,000-plus studios off the platform entirely. Photographers' own economics are thin: freelancers are reported at **NPR 10,000–50,000 per event** and staff photographers at NPR 25,000–45,000 per month ([KumariJob](https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/career-resources/how-to-find-photographer-job-nepal)), against camera bodies priced from **NPR 76,000 (Canon R100) to NPR 370,000 (R5)** ([Gadgetbyte](https://www.gadgetbytenepal.com/canon-camera-price-nepal/)) that, in one photographer's words, must be upgraded "every two to three years because they get outdated" ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/art-culture/2025/02/24/nepal-photography-industry-is-unkind-to-newcomers)). Gear alone plausibly eats 10–25% of a mid-earner's gross. There is no national headcount of photographers; the "Nepalese in Photography" Facebook group reports **more than 293,800 members** but is a hobbyist community ([Nepalese Photography](https://www.nepalesephotography.com/)), the National Forum of Photo Journalists has 500-plus members across 35 districts ([Rising Nepal](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/67123)), and the Kathmandu Photographer Association requires a business registration and PAN to join ([KPA](https://kpa.org.np/)), which means the semi-pro long tail a marketplace would aggregate is invisible to any official count. Recruiting hobbyist supply would be easy; recruiting and keeping the professionals clients actually want would not.

The off-season filler the founder is counting on, business and content work, is real but small-ticket. Statista sizes Nepal's entire digital ad market at about **USD 71 million in 2024 and influencer advertising at roughly USD 4 million** ([Gurkha Tech](https://gurkhatech.com/nepal-digital-advertising-2025-outlook/)); influencer campaigns run NPR 1,500–150,000 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2023/07/26/influencer-marketers-leave-celebrities-behind)); and restaurant social-media packages sit at NPR 25,000–50,000 a month including everything ([Reverhub](https://reverhub.com/blog/details/social-media-marketing-cost-in-nepal-2026)). A per-shoot budget derived from those retainers is a few thousand rupees, an order of magnitude below a wedding day, and the SMB clients churn: **half of new Kathmandu restaurants shut within 12 months** ([Katmandu Journal](https://katmandujournal.com/2026/08/03/half-of-new-restaurants-in-kathmandu-shut-down-within-12-months/)). Thirty-one influencer agencies plus UGC shops like Task n Trade and creator marketplaces such as CreatorKhoj already compete for this money ([TechBehemoths](https://techbehemoths.com/companies/influencer-advertising/nepal); [CreatorKhoj](https://creatorkhoj.com/)). Off-season B2B demand can smooth a photographer's year; it cannot carry the platform's economics.

Infrastructure is not the constraint. Mobile broadband penetration is **95% of population by subscriptions** ([NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2026/11/nepal-broadband-penetration-reaches-148-64-in-fy-2082-83.html)), though unique internet users are only about 16.5 million, roughly 56% of people ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-nepal)); electronic payments reached **NPR 98.43 trillion in FY 2024/25, up about 71%** ([New Business Age](https://www.newbusinessage.com/news/45196/digital-transactions-surge--nepals-electronic-payments-nearly-triple-in-five-years/)), with QR transaction counts up 92.5% ([Himal Press](https://en.himalpress.com/qr-payments-drive-digital-payment-boom-number-of-transactions-up-92-5/)). Studios already take eSewa and Khalti advances for bookings ([WebApp Nepal](https://webappnepal.com/service/photography)). The behavioural gap is narrower than it looks: Nepalis will pay a studio an advance by wallet, and Sajilo Sewa has shown Kathmandu, Pokhara and Butwal households will book a vetted stranger through an app for a home repair, claiming 280,000 households served ([Sajilo Sewa](https://sajilosewa.com/)). What remains unproven is whether they will prepay a platform, rather than a known studio, for a date-critical, taste-dependent NPR 40,000 service; e-commerce SMEs still report cash on delivery above 50% of orders ([Arjan KC](https://arjankc.com.np/blog/nepal-digital-economy-report-2026-preview/)).

Finally, the competitive field in Nepal is empty in a way that should worry rather than excite. Bihe Bazaar, WedTayari, Photopasal, MeroNajik and Nepal Photo Hub all list photographers, but none takes the transaction: Bihe Bazaar's own FAQ says it "is not involved in any transaction... you are directly contacting the vendor" ([Bihe Bazaar FAQ](https://www.bihebazaar.com/faq)), and despite shipping an iOS app its Instagram shows **313 followers** ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/bihebazaar/)). Horizontal freelance boards (MeroKaam, Yaha Chha, Freelanced Nepal) are small or in beta ([Founders Nepal](https://foundersnepal.com/product/freelanced-nepal)), and the one scaled services app, Sajilo Sewa, does not list photographers. Nobody runs client-posted jobs with bids. Photographers already have Instagram and "DM to book" ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/wedding_nepal_photography/)); they do not need another brochure. If the unmet need exists, it is on the transaction and trust side (deposits, date holds, no-show replacement, dispute handling), not on discovery.

## No photographer marketplace anywhere survived on open bidding

The founder's mental model is "Fiverr or Upwork, but local." The track record says the relevant comparison set is on-demand photography platforms and local services marketplaces, and their verdict is consistent.

### The well-funded platforms all set prices themselves, and still struggled

Meero raised a **USD 230 million round in 2019** to become France's fifth unicorn ([Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/230-millions-meero/)), ran a fixed-price managed marketplace for B2B photography, and from 11 February 2023 stopped taking photo-shoot bookings altogether ([Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Meero)); the survivor, Diffusely, is an AI-visuals software company with €12 million ARR from 1,500 fashion and automotive clients ([FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/diffusely-ex-meero-rebondit-sur-son-pivot-et-leve-11-millions-deuros/457692)). Its supply-side failure is instructive: a Belgian photographer reported a one-hour real-estate shoot "generally paid €30" and professionals called it the "Uber of photography" for its rates ([L'ADN](https://www.ladn.eu/tech-a-suivre/meero-photographe-precarisation/amp/)). Kodak's Kodakit shut after about two years amid weak uptake, a copyright grab and industry pushback that it was "lowering the value of photography services" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2019/12/12/kodak-shuttering-kodakit-the-uber-of-photography/)). Snappr survived, raising a Series B in October 2025 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/08/snappr-photographer-book-europe-launch)), but only by publishing one fixed price per country across 484 cities and charging clients **15% pay-as-you-go, 9% with a membership, and nothing on enterprise accounts** needing 30-plus shoots a month ([Snappr pricing](https://www.snappr.com/photography-pricing); [Snappr enterprise](https://www.snappr.com/enterprise-editing)); its photographers meanwhile reported a 20–35% commission and pay "way below minimum wage" when shoots overran ([Inside Imaging](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/snappr-experience-low-pay-and-unrealistic-expectations/)), and its client reviews recurrently cite no-shows ([ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/snappr)). The small consumer survivors changed the unit instead: Shoott sells free 30-minute sessions and charges **USD 25 per photo** while guaranteeing photographers USD 100 per hour ([Shoott FAQ](https://www.shoott.com/faq)). Not one of these used open bidding, and the two that scaled fastest concluded that one-off consumer and SMB shoots do not carry a business; both re-anchored on repeat corporate volume that Nepal does not have.

### The closest bidding precedent abandoned bidding because jobs stopped completing

TaskRabbit is the single most relevant case for a bid-based local services marketplace. In June 2014 it found that **while both sides were growing, its task-completion rate was dropping**, and diagnosed inefficient matching ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/17/following-a-drop-in-completed-jobs-errands-marketplace-taskrabbit-shakes-up-its-business-model)); it replaced bidding with fixed hourly rates in July 2014, over loud tasker backlash ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/taskrabbit-pivots-launches-on-demand-service-for-cleaning-handiwork-moving-errands)). Case writers describe the fix as creating "an effective floor on the dollar value of a given task" that avoided a "race-to-the-bottom" ([Harvard D^3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/submission/taskrabbit-your-retinue-of-errand-runners)), and fixed pricing "finally brought utilization high enough that their margins could recover" ([BIA](https://www.bia.com/blog/how-taskrabbit-defied-on-demand-logic-to-reach-breakeven/)). Today taskers keep 100% of their rate and clients pay the fees ([TaskRabbit](https://www.taskrabbit.com/blog/how-pricing-works-on-taskrabbit-and-what-it-means-for-your-earnings/)). Airtasker still runs open bidding with a roughly 14.5% supplier fee, but in a high-income market with a generic task mix ([TheWearify](https://thewearify.com/airtasker-vs-taskrabbit/)), and Freelancer.com, the pure bidding site at 10%, was outgrown by fixed-menu Fiverr at a flat 20% ([Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/upwork-vs-fiverr-vs-freelancer-com-vs-zero-commission-platforms-complete-2025-comparison/)).

### India's survivors sell listings, not bookings

Across the border, the wedding-tech companies that lived are subscription directories. WedMeGood is free for couples and earns **80% of revenue from vendor subscriptions** ([Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/lifestyle/wedmegood-making-your-dream-wedding-a-hassle-free-affair/250744)), reaching FY25 revenue variously reported at ₹34–45 crore ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/wedmegood/)); ShaadiSaga sold to Matrimony.com for Rs 11 crore ([NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/MATRIMONY_07072021123400_NSEmediarelease07072021_0001.pdf)) and Weddingz to OYO, which wanted the venues ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=133041)). Companies that tried to manage or productise photography fared worse: Info Edge wrote off its entire **~Rs 1,211 million** investment in Canvera ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/companies/naukri-com-owner-info-edge-churns-its-portfolio-sells-canvera-to-printo-118080101587_1.html)), and Urban Company, having once offered 97 categories, wound up photography and wedding services to concentrate on beauty and home where it could "manage and monitor service quality closely" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/would-be-unicorn-urbanclap-decides-to-operate-in-only-2-service-categories); [DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/urbanclap-non-core-businesses-159072)). Pakistan's Shadiyana raised an USD 800,000 pre-seed in December 2025 as a wedding directory whose primary revenue driver is marriage halls, not photographers ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/09/shadiyana-raises-800000-pre-seed-to-digitize-pakistans-rs-900-billion-wedding-industry/amp/)). Photographers are the low-value tail on every South Asian wedding platform, and no Indian company was found that ever ran open bidding for them. The lead-gen model that works in the US does not port either: Thumbtack photographers pay **USD 20–75 per lead** whether or not they win ([LeadCapture](https://leadcapture.io/blog/thumbtack-lead-cost/)), one reports USD 90 leads for USD 250 jobs ([Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/6255)), and that arithmetic is impossible against Nepali tickets.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meero (FR) | Fixed-price managed, B2B | Marketplace closed Feb 2023; pivoted to SaaS | Photographer pay of €30/hr left no supply loyalty |
| Snappr (AU/US) | Fixed national price, client fee 15/9/0% | Alive via enterprise repeat volume | Repeat buyers, not matching, were the scarce asset |

### "Cheapest wins" strips out the signal clients actually use

The founder's central mechanism contradicts what the data on labour auctions shows buyers do. A study of about 1.8 million Freelancer.com bids found open, visible bids beat sealed bids on contract rate and satisfaction precisely because visible bids let weak bidders screen themselves out ([ERIM](https://www.erim.eur.nl/research/events/detail/3111/)); in buyer-determined auctions "the lowest-priced bidder is not the default winner" because cheap sellers may deliver low quality, abscond with advances or delay ([freelance job-market paper](https://rebeccawu96.github.io/RW_Econ_JM/jmp/freelance_jmp_latest.pdf)); and on the same platform **price dispersion lowers buyers' willingness to contract while quality dispersion raises it** ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/301365736.pdf)). Longer, looser auctions attract more bids but also more low-quality, inexperienced bidders ([U. Hawaii](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/cf4de34e-9ca1-4c97-a782-62c6af40e51f/full)). Forcing the lowest bid to win removes the screening clients rely on, so the model should expect lower, not higher, conversion. Nepal's supply already shows the symptom: a studio owner complains that "the clients don't understand the criteria of pricing" and that newcomers at very low rates squeeze established studios, while Fotopasal caps itself at three events per date to protect quality ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html); [OnlineKhabar on Fotopasal](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/wedding-photography-nepal-fotopasal.html)). A cheapest-wins platform recruits the undercutters and repels the Fotopasals, and a wedding, unlike a Freelancer.com logo, cannot be re-done when the cheap bidder no-shows.

### Leakage will be near-total on first contact

The second structural problem is disintermediation, the industry word for both sides meeting through you once and then cutting you out. A randomised trial on a freelance marketplace found that as a platform builds trust between the sides, circumvention to avoid fees rises ([INFORMS, Gu & Zhu](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583)); a follow-up found leakage highest for time-sensitive, communication-intensive, high-skill jobs, and blocking one hard-to-monitor channel cut it by about 18% ([RePEc, Gu 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:70:y:2024:i:11:p:7868-7891)). Estimates range from **about 90% of matched transactions leaking** on some service platforms to one in 83 on a home-cleaning app ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-disintermediation)). Wedding photography is the worst case on every axis: communication-heavy, date-critical, high-stakes, and in Nepal already conducted over phone, Messenger and eSewa. A pure matching layer that charges a transaction commission will be used as a free lead source and closed off-platform. Marketplace theory says the same from the other direction: platforms that do little beyond matching can justify only a thin take rate, and higher take rates come from added value such as trust, guarantees and logistics ([Rosetta/a16z](https://rosetta.to/u/a16z/marketplace-breakout-strategies-take-rates-defensibility-payments-field-notes-by)). Nepal's own gig history sets the ceiling: Pathao's **20% commission triggered a December 2020 rider protest, office vandalism and 26 arrests** ([Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/discontented-pathao-riders-vandalise-companys-office)), inDrive entered at 10% ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/05/07/indrive-launches-officially-in-nepal)), and by August 2026 Pathao had replaced percentage commission with a fixed Rs 12 platform fee ([ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/pathao-backs-fair-rider-earnings-calls-for-common-fare-rules-2026-08-23)). A Fiverr-style 20% is above every local reference point; 10% or a client-side fee is the defensible band, and at 10% of a NPR 40,000 booking you earn NPR 4,000 per one-time customer, out of which you must fund acquisition, disputes and no-show cover. A bootstrapped comparable listed for sale after ten years showed where that ends: 1,200 photographers, about 800 bookings and roughly **USD 1,900 a month in profit** (seller-provided figures) ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/11905485-profitable-10-yr-online-platform-with-30-40-margins-1-9k-monthly-profit-1-200-photographers-800-bookings-and-strong-seo-growth-potential)).

## Nepal launches in weeks; India and "worldwide" hit legal walls

Every figure in this section comes from secondary sources (law-firm blogs, Nepali and Indian business press), because the researchers could not open primary statute or circular text; verify each with a Nepali chartered accountant and lawyer before relying on it. With that caveat, the picture is unusually clear-cut.

### Nepal: hassles and costs, no blockers, with one tax trap

Incorporating a private limited company costs a government fee of **NPR 1,000 to 16,000** depending on authorised capital ([Bizsewa](https://bizsewa.com/?p=143)) and takes roughly 7–21 days ([Nepal Legal Firm](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/company-registration-process-nepal-2025)). The one new obligation founders routinely miss is the **Electronic Commerce Act, 2081**, certified 16 March 2025, which applies to anyone selling goods or services within Nepal by digital means ([Law Bhandari](https://lawbhandari.com/publication/e-commerce-law-in-nepal)): the platform must be listed with the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection and display its listing number, PAN and complaint contact before trading ([Law Alpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/e-commerce-act-nepal-2025)), with non-registration fines reported at up to NPR 200,000 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/07/08/nepal-s-online-sellers-must-register-or-pay-up-to-rs200-000-fine)) and unlisted platforms facing cancellation under the February 2026 Directive ([CollegeNP](https://www.collegenp.com/news/unregistered-e-commerce-platforms-face-cancellation)). Commentators note the Act is ambiguous on how intermediaries are treated and lacks a clear safe harbour ([B360](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/25494/the-e-commerce-act-2081-a-legal-milestone-or-a-maze-of-confusion)), so the terms of service must frame the platform as an agent between client and photographer and run a documented complaint and refund process.

Tax is where the founder has missed something expensive. The platform's commission attracts **13% VAT**, with registration mandatory above NPR 30 lakh of annual services turnover ([Law Alpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/vat-in-nepal-rates-and-thresholds-2082-83)); that is a cost, not a problem. The trap is withholding. Under Income Tax Act s.88, when the platform pays a photographer it must withhold **15% if the photographer is not VAT-registered, and 1.5% if they are and issue a VAT invoice** ([IRD rate table](https://www.ird.gov.np/public/pdf/1747549774.pdf); [Niti Partners](https://nitipartners.com/?p=9872)). Almost every freelancer and small studio is below the VAT threshold, so routing payments through the platform means every photographer sees 15% vanish before your commission is even counted, compared with 0.1% for the equivalent Indian rule. This is the single biggest tax issue for the model and argues for a structure where the client pays the photographer directly and the platform charges only its own fee, or for lobbying for a sector concession like ride-share's 1% TDS ([NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/ride-sharing-to-be-taxed-from-shrawan-1-fares-set-to-rise)). The ride-share precedent also cuts the other way: from July 2026 platforms collect 5% VAT on the full fare plus 13% on their commission ([LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/nepal/ride-sharing-platform-vat-2026)), proof the IRD is willing to tax gross flows sector by sector once a category gets large.

Escrow is the one genuine Nepal-side blocker, and it matters because a deposit hold is exactly the trust feature this market needs. Nepal's licensing policy recognises only Payment Service Providers (wallets) and Payment System Operators (switches), both requiring a Letter of Intent and infrastructure inspection ([Pradhan Law](https://pradhanlaw.com/publications/licensing-policy-for-institutions-that-perform-payment-related-work-2079-2023-ad)), and NRB has been revoking licences, not issuing them ([NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/nrb-revokes-licenses-of-prabhu-technology-and-first-pay-technology)). There is no payment-aggregator or marketplace-escrow category. What every Nepali platform does instead is collect into its own merchant account via eSewa, Khalti or Fonepay at roughly 1–3.5% ([PaymentProviders.io](https://paymentproviders.io/providers/esewa.md); [Merokalam](https://merokalam.pages.dev/blog/online-business-registration-nepal/)), hold the money as ordinary company funds under contractual "release on delivery" terms, and pay out by bank transfer after TDS. That works, but client money is then an unsecured liability of your company, and the only formal path to a segregated hold is NRB's Regulatory Sandbox, whose June 2026 guidelines let unlicensed fintechs test with a partner bank ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/06/24/regulatory-sandbox-guidelines-aim-to-foster-nepal-fintech-innovation)). Foreign clients paying a Nepali entity are a hassle, not a blocker: PayPal cannot pay out to Nepal and native Stripe requires a foreign entity ([Merokalam](https://merokalam.pages.dev/blog/paypal-account-in-nepal/)), but **Khalti–Stripe (April 2026) accepts international cards at a 2% fee** with automatic NPR conversion ([Khalti FAQ](https://blog.khalti.com/offers/frequently-asked-questions-receive-international-payments-directly-in-your-khalti-wallet-via-stripe/)), and IT-service export income enjoyed a 75% income-tax exemption in FY 2082/83 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/05/30/budget-2025-26-introduces-sweeping-tax-incentives-for-tech-exports-startups-and-green-hydrogen-firms-while-raising-duties-on-alcohol-tobacco-and-luxury-goods)), reportedly trimmed to 50% for FY 2083/84 ([Law Alpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/budget-2083-84-tax-changes-in-nepal)).

### India: three independent walls

The plan to "launch in Bangalore next" fails on money going out of Nepal, money going into an Indian company, and money paid to Indian photographers, each independently. First, the Act Restricting Investment Abroad, 2021 (1964) prohibits Nepalis from investing abroad except by gazette exemption ([Nepal Law Commission](https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13402/restriction-act--2021/)); the NRB's Fifth Amendment of 30 December 2025 loosened this to allow any Nepali company to invest **up to USD 20,000 abroad without prior approval** ([Mondaq / Niti Partners](https://www.mondaq.com/inward-foreign-investment/1727272/nrb-eases-equity-inflows-repatriation-and-outward-investment)), which does not capitalise an Indian operation. Second, India's Press Note 3 of 2020 requires prior Government of India approval, through a Home-Secretary-led inter-ministerial committee, for investment from any land-border country including Nepal ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/inward-foreign-investment/926414/foreign-direct-investment-in-india-impact-of-press-note-3-of-2020)); the March 2026 easing opened the automatic route only for stakes **below 10%** ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/cabinet-eases-press-note-3-rules-allows-up-to-10-border-country-fdi-through-automatic-route)), useless for a controlling subsidiary. Third, there is no rail to pay hundreds of Indian freelancers from Nepal: Fonepay–UPI (March 2024) lets Indians pay Nepali merchants, one way ([Fonepay](https://fonepay.com/blogs/upi-in-nepal)); the NCHL–NPCI UPI–NPI link live from 9 June 2026 is **person-to-person remittance** through select banks ([NCHL](https://nchl.com.np/npci-international-and-nepal-clearing-house-launch-real-time-cross-border-remittance-link-between-india-and-nepal/)); Nepali cardholders are capped at IRs 100,000 a month in India ([myRepublica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/card-payments-in-india-for-nepalis-limited-to-irs-100-000-a-month)); and NRB and RBI were still negotiating an interbank channel for large INR transfers in February 2026 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/02/03/nepal-india-explore-interbank-channel-for-large-indian-currency-transfers)). If an Indian company funded by Indian partners did exist, it would register for GST as an e-commerce operator regardless of turnover, collect 0.5% TCS under s.52 ([GST Gyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/section-52-collection-of-tax-at-source-tcs-under-gst)), deduct 0.1% TDS under s.194-O ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/budget-2024-tds-rate-for-ecommerce-operators-cut-to-0-1-from-1)) and build DPDP consent and breach processes before the core rules bite around May 2027 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)); all of that is manageable. The point is that India requires Indian capital and an Indian partner, not a Nepali subsidiary.

The pattern holds for "worldwide." Every Nepali-origin company that raised at scale abroad was foreign-incorporated with a founder already abroad: Fusemachines listed on Nasdaq via SPAC from New York ([New Business Age](https://www.newbusinessage.com/news/46195/fusemachines-debuts-on-nasdaq/)), CloudFactory was founded by a Canadian and raised USD 65 million ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/cloudfactory-nepal-layoffs/)), and Lamina Labs raised its USD 3 million seed in San Francisco ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/135333-nepali-founders-raise-3m-seed-for-ai-explainer-video-startup-lamina-labs/)); a critic observes that Nepal's successes are registered abroad ([Katmandu Journal](https://katmandujournal.com/2026/04/19/why-nepali-startups-struggle-to-become-companies/)). A Nepal-resident founder cannot legally fund a Delaware or Singapore holding company past USD 20,000, and a Nepali entity cannot hold a USD float to pay foreign photographers because inbound foreign currency must settle in NPR ([Techmandu](https://techmandu.com/nepali-businesses-can-receive-foreign-currency-on-digital-wallets)). "Global photographers for global clients" is therefore not available to this founder from Nepal; "Nepali photographers and editors for global clients" is.

| Segregated escrow for client deposits | Blocker | Only PSP/PSO licences exist; sandbox is the sole route |
| Paying Indian or other foreign photographers from Nepal | Blocker | FX outflows restricted; UPI–NPI is P2P; card cap IRs 100,000/month |

Funding is the last thing the founder should size realistically. Nepali pre-seed rounds run about NPR 30 lakh to 1 crore and seed NPR 1–5 crore ([Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/nepali-startup-funding-2026/)); Nepal Angel Network's three deals in the first half of 2026 ranged from USD 50,000 to a USD 120,000 follow-on ([Nepal Database](https://www.nepaldatabase.com/nepal-startup-funding-in-2026-whats-verified-whats-pending-and-where-founders-should-look)); the government Startup Enterprise Loan lends up to NPR 25 lakh at 3% with the project as collateral, and drew 5,158 applications for a NPR 1 billion pool ([ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=60607); [OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/?p=566586)). Foreign VC into a Nepali company faces a NPR 20 million minimum per investor and convertible notes that NRB has refused to recognise ([Common Law Nepal](https://commonlaw.com.np/publications/fitta-regulations-nepal); [B360](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/10278/graduating-from-ldc-to-an-emerging-investment-hub-challenges-and-reforms-for-nepal)). Asset-light consumer platforms are also where Nepal's graveyard is deepest: Sastodeal closed in 2024 with about 200 jobs lost despite a USD 1 million Dolma cheque ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/04/14/back-to-physical-stores-e-commerce-falters-in-nepal-s-topsy-turvy-business-climate)), while the survivors (Foodmandu, Upaya, Pathao) own a physical operation or a regulated rail. This business must be built to work on NPR 50 lakh to 1 crore of patient local money, not on a Series A.

## Rank the assumptions most likely to kill it, then test each for under NPR 1 lakh

No source in the research establishes how Nepali clients choose photographers, how many would prepay a platform, or what share of a studio's year is idle. Those unknowns, not the regulatory items, decide whether a business exists. The founder has an asset most first-time founders lack: a working studio whose booking diary, turned-away inquiries and monthly revenue are already evidence. Ranked by how likely each assumption is to be false and how fatal it would be:

| Rank | Assumption | Evidence against it so far | Cheapest test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clients will pick the cheapest bidder for a wedding | Buyers on bidding platforms do not default to the lowest bid; price dispersion lowers contracting ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/301365736.pdf)); a wedding cannot be re-shot | Concierge test: a Facebook page and WhatsApp number in the hometown and Kathmandu offering "tell us your date and budget, get three quotes in 24 hours"; broker 20–30 jobs by hand; record whether the cheapest quote wins, and how often clients ask for portfolios first. Cost: ad spend at NPR 500–1,000/day ([Epitome Solutions](https://epitomesolutions.org/blog/digital-marketing-nepal/)) plus your time |
| 2 | Quality photographers will bid on, and stay on, a platform built around undercutting | Studios already resent newcomers' low rates ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)); Pathao's 20% provoked a riot ([Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/discontented-pathao-riders-vandalise-companys-office)) | Interview 15 working photographers recruited through KPA and the Nepalese in Photography group. Offer three deals, 10% commission, 15%, and a flat monthly listing fee, and ask which they would accept on a real Falgun date. Ask directly whether they would bid against peers on price |
| 3 | Bookings will stay on-platform rather than closing over Messenger | Leakage is highest for time-sensitive, communication-heavy jobs ([Gu 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:70:y:2024:i:11:p:7868-7891)); "DM to book" is the norm | In the concierge test, take an eSewa deposit into the company account on half the jobs and simply introduce the parties on the other half; count how many of the second group pay you anything |
| 4 | The family studio's seasonality problem is representative and solvable with off-season B2B work | Influencer market ~USD 4 million; per-shoot SMB budgets of a few thousand rupees ([Gurkha Tech](https://gurkhatech.com/nepal-digital-advertising-2025-outlook/)) | Free: pull three years of the studio's revenue by month and count peak-date inquiries turned away. Then cold-call 50 hometown restaurants and shops with a NPR 5,000–15,000 reels-and-product shoot; measure conversion and whether any buy twice |
| 5 | Clients will prepay a platform, not a known studio, for a future-dated service | COD still above 50% of e-commerce orders ([Arjan KC](https://arjankc.com.np/blog/nepal-digital-economy-report-2026-preview/)); but studios already collect eSewa advances ([WebApp Nepal](https://webappnepal.com/service/photography)) | A one-page site with a "date-lock deposit via eSewa, photographer guaranteed or replaced" offer; measure deposit conversion from NPR 20,000 of ads before writing any software |
| 6 | Peak-date overflow can be cleared to second-tier shooters without quality complaints | Fotopasal caps itself at 3 events per date for quality ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/wedding-photography-nepal-fotopasal.html)); Snappr reviews cite no-shows | On the studio's next fully booked saait dates, refer turned-away clients to three vetted peers for a referral fee; collect a satisfaction call a week later |
| 7 | Nepal → India → worldwide is an executable sequence | Three legal walls (above) | Do not test; drop it from the plan and revisit if the NRB–RBI interbank INR channel and a merchant leg of UPI–NPI go live |

Run tests 1 through 4 before incorporating anything. Together they cost less than one mid-tier wedding package and will tell the founder within one wedding season whether the business is a platform, a service, or nothing. The signal to look for in test 1 is not "did clients like getting quotes" but "did the cheapest quote win more than a third of the time without the client asking to see work first." If it did not, the auction is dead and the pivot below is the product.

## Keep the studio, drop the auction: what to build instead

The honest verdict is that the founder has found a real pain and attached the wrong mechanism, the wrong geography and the wrong revenue model to it. The version of the idea that the evidence supports looks like this.

Replace open bidding with curated matching inside a price band. The client posts the date, location and a budget tier; the platform shows two or three vetted photographers with fixed packages at or near that tier, portfolios first, and the client chooses. Prices are set or floored by tier, the way Snappr publishes one price per country ([Snappr pricing](https://www.snappr.com/photography-pricing)) and TaskRabbit put "an effective floor" under tasks ([Harvard D^3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/submission/taskrabbit-your-retinue-of-errand-runners)). Photographers compete on work and reliability, not on who will go below NPR 15,000. This keeps the Fotopasal-tier studios on the platform, which is what gives a wedding client a reason to trust it.

Monetise the thing the market lacks, which is a trustworthy hold on a date, not a cheaper photographer. Charge a date-lock deposit fee or a modest client-side service fee for a guarantee that the photographer shows up or is replaced from the vetted pool; that is value a Messenger DM cannot provide and the only kind of fee that survives leakage. Add a flat monthly or seasonal listing subscription for photographers, the model that produced **80% of WedMeGood's revenue** ([Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/lifestyle/wedmegood-making-your-dream-wedding-a-hassle-free-affair/250744)) and that India's directories chose precisely because per-transaction commissions leak. Keep any commission at 10% or below, given inDrive's 10% and Pathao's retreat to a flat fee ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/05/07/indrive-launches-officially-in-nepal); [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/pathao-backs-fair-rider-earnings-calls-for-common-fare-rules-2026-08-23)). Have clients pay photographers directly where possible so the 15% TDS does not land on every payout, and confirm that structure with an accountant before the first booking.

Use the family studio as the managed layer, not as a customer. Nepal's survivors owned an operation; its asset-light aggregators died. A studio that vets peers, holds the deposit, supplies a backup shooter on a Falgun date and quality-checks the delivered gallery is the physical anchor that makes a platform credible in one city. Start with the hometown and one more cluster where pricing data already exists (Kathmandu valley or Pokhara), and design the peak-date overflow network first, because that is where the excess demand is measurable: 60–70 weddings on a date that each studio can cover six of ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)). Fill the off-season deliberately with event, corporate and small-business shoots, but budget them as a smoothing line for photographers, not as platform revenue; the money is in the 41 dates.

Replace "then India, then worldwide" with "then export Nepali labour." The one cross-border story that works from a Nepali entity is foreign demand for Nepali editing and content work: Kathmandu wedding-film editors already sell on Upwork at USD 30 an hour to clients in the UK, US and Canada ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/video-editors/np/kathmandu/)), Khalti–Stripe collects foreign card payments at 2% into NPR ([Khalti FAQ](https://blog.khalti.com/offers/frequently-asked-questions-receive-international-payments-directly-in-your-khalti-wallet-via-stripe/)), and IT-export income carries a 50–75% tax exemption. A Nepal-only platform whose photographers also edit for foreign studios in the off-season is legal today, counter-seasonal by design and tax-advantaged. A Bangalore launch requires an Indian partner with Indian capital and should wait until the UPI–NPI link grows a business leg; a "global" marketplace requires a foreign holding company this founder cannot legally fund.

Finally, set up the company to be boring and compliant from day one: a Nepali Pvt Ltd with authorised capital under NPR 25 lakh (an NPR 9,500 OCR fee), PAN and voluntary VAT registration, the Department of Commerce e-commerce listing displayed on the site, eSewa, Khalti and Fonepay merchant accounts plus Khalti–Stripe, agent-framed terms of service with a written complaint and refund process, and monthly e-TDS filing. None of that is hard; skipping it is how Nepali platforms get fined or cancelled under the 2026 Directive.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the opportunity from "Fiverr for photographers in Nepal" to "a trust layer for forty days a year." Every piece of evidence points the same way: clients do not want the cheapest shooter, photographers will not stay on a platform that makes them cheaper, the transaction will leave the platform the moment the two sides have a phone number, and no on-demand photography company anywhere has made money on one-off consumer shoots. What survives those facts is narrow but real: a curated network with a deposit-backed date guarantee, floors instead of auctions, subscriptions instead of commissions, and a studio at its centre that does the vetting a software layer cannot. That business will be small by venture standards, perhaps a comfortable regional company rather than a unicorn, and it must be funded on NPR 50 lakh to 1 crore of patient local money.

The deeper lesson for a first-time founder is about sequencing. The regulatory blockers to India and the world are not reasons to despair; they are a gift, because they force the plan onto the one geography where the founder has an unfair advantage and the one experiment that costs almost nothing: the studio's own booking diary. If three years of that diary show peak-date inquiries being turned away and five dead months, the overflow-and-deposit product is worth building, and the concierge test will show within one wedding season whether anyone pays for it. If the diary shows a studio dying of smartphones, the marketplace is the wrong rescue, and the family business needs a different plan.
