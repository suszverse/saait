# Regulatory, tax, payments and cross-border constraints: Nepal photographer/creator marketplace (Nepal first, then India, then global)

Research date: 7 November 2026. Method note: web search summaries only. Direct fetches of nrb.org.np, ird.gov.np, kathmandupost.com, sharesansar.com, ictframe.com and every Nepali law-firm site were blocked by this session's network allowlist, so **no primary statute, circular or gazette text was read directly**. Every figure below is from secondary reporting (law-firm blogs, Nepali tech/business press, Indian tax portals) and is flagged accordingly. Nepali dates: FY 2082/83 = mid-July 2025 to mid-July 2026; FY 2083/84 = mid-July 2026 to mid-July 2027. "Shrawan 1" ≈ 16/17 July.

Classification used throughout: **[BLOCKER]** = legal prohibition or licence you cannot get as a startup; **[HASSLE]** = compliance step that takes time/paperwork but is achievable; **[COST]** = fee or tax drag.

## 1. Company setup in Nepal: OCR process, cost, time; PAN/VAT thresholds; Electronic Commerce Act 2081 and the Department of Commerce listing; consumer-protection obligations

### Takeaway
Incorporating a Nepali private limited company is cheap (government fee NPR 1,000–16,000 depending on authorised capital) and fast (roughly 1–3 weeks). The real new obligation is the **Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (certified 16 March 2025, in force ~mid-April 2025)** plus the **Electronic Commerce Directive, 2082 (final text released 18 February 2026)**: any platform selling goods *or services* electronically must be listed on the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection portal, display its listing number, PAN and complaint contact, and honour consumer-protection duties; unlisted platforms face fines (reported up to NPR 200,000–500,000) and cancellation. This is a [HASSLE], not a blocker.

### Cited Findings

**Company registration (Office of the Company Registrar, OCR)**
- Online registration of a new private company typically takes about 7–10 days — [Niti Partners](https://nitipartners.com/online-company-registration-in-nepal-2026-updated/); another guide says 7–21 days depending on document accuracy and OCR workload — [Nepal Legal Firm](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/company-registration-process-nepal-2025); an IT-company-specific guide says 3–7 working days — [Niti Partners IT registration](https://nitipartners.com/it-company-registration-in-nepal/).
- Government fee is tiered by **authorised** (not paid-up) capital — [Kumari Job](https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/general-information/how-to-register-a-company-in-nepal). FY 2082/83 private-company table: up to NPR 100,000 → NPR 1,000; 100,001–500,000 → NPR 4,500; 500,001–2,500,000 → NPR 9,500; 2,500,001–1 crore → NPR 16,000 — [Bizsewa FY 2082/83](https://bizsewa.com/?p=143). The same private-company bands appear in a 2022 Pioneer Law table (dated 8/10/2022), which also lists public-company fees (up to 1 crore → NPR 15,000; 1–10 crore → NPR 40,000) — [Pioneer Law company guide PDF](https://pioneerlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/company-jun29.pdf).
- A 2025 comparison estimates NPR 8,000–15,000 total government fees for a typical private limited — [TaxVAT Nepal](https://taxvatnepal.com/?p=941). Expect additional professional/notary costs on top.
- Sequence: name reservation → online filing of MoA/AoA → OCR certificate → PAN (and VAT if applicable) registration with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) → ward/municipality business registration — [Nepal Legal Firm](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/company-registration-process-nepal-2025); [Necojobs](https://www.necojobs.com.np/blogs/business-tips/how-to-start-a-company-in-nepal).

**VAT registration thresholds**
- Current VAT thresholds: NPR 50 lakh (5 million) annual turnover for goods, **NPR 30 lakh (3 million) for services**, the services figure having been raised from an earlier NPR 20 lakh by Finance Act 2081 and carried into FY 2082/83 — [Law Alpine VAT 2082/83](https://lawalpine.com/blog/vat-in-nepal-rates-and-thresholds-2082-83); [Bizsewa VAT FY 2082/83](https://bizsewa.com/?p=807). Older sources still quote 20 lakh — [Trykintsugi Nepal VAT guide](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/apac/nepal). Standard rate 13%.
- Non-resident digital service providers have a separate regime: 2% Digital Service Tax (DST) on transaction value, plus VAT registration above NPR 2 million (~USD 15,800) annual sales to Nepali consumers, from July 2022 — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2022/11/04/digital-service-tax-dst-and-value-added-tax-vat-registration-for-digital-service-provided-by-non-resident-person); [Quaderno](https://quaderno.io/guides/nepal/vat/); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-international/nepal-tax-agency-posts-notice-on-digital-service-tax-vat-for-nonresident-taxpayers). (Relevant only if the platform were run from abroad.)

**Electronic Commerce Act, 2081 (2025)**
- The Act was certified by the President on 16 March 2025 and came into force on the 31st day after certification; it applies to all individuals and entities, regardless of physical location, that sell or deliver goods **and services** within Nepal by digital means — [News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/nepal-passes-e-commerce-bill-to-regulate-online-trade/); [Law Bhandari](https://lawbhandari.com/publication/e-commerce-law-in-nepal). Another law-firm summary gives Gazette publication March 2025 and in-force mid-April 2025 (Baisakh 2, 2082) — [Law Alpine E-Commerce Act](https://lawalpine.com/blog/e-commerce-act-nepal-2025); [Hamro Patro](https://www.hamropatro.com/en/news/detail/4639130139724541-running-an-online-business-in-nepal-registration-is-mandatory-unregistered).
- Section 5: platforms must register/list electronically with the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection after company incorporation; Section 4: the platform must display business name, address, registration details, PAN, contact/customer-service details and a complaint contact — [Law Alpine E-Commerce Act](https://lawalpine.com/blog/e-commerce-act-nepal-2025). The listing number must be displayed before trading — [Law Alpine registration guide](https://lawalpine.com/blog/registration-of-e-commerce-business-in-nepal).
- The Department set a mandatory registration deadline of Shrawan 2, 2082 (≈18 July 2025) with a penalty of up to NPR 200,000 for non-registration — [Kathmandu Post, 8 July 2025](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/07/08/nepal-s-online-sellers-must-register-or-pay-up-to-rs200-000-fine); [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/mandatory-e-commerce-business-registration-by-shrawan-2).
- Penalty range under the Act reported as roughly NPR 20,000 to NPR 500,000 with imprisonment up to three years for serious violations by platform operators — [CEOTab](https://ceotab.com/e-commerce-act-2081-key-provisions-and-implementation-timeline/). One blog reports not honouring a stated refund/return policy carries NPR 50,000–300,000 — [Nepal Legal Firm consumer refund](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/consumer-protection-refund-laws-nepal) (unverified against Act text).
- The Act requires e-commerce businesses to protect personal information and let users amend it — [DLA Piper Data Protection: Nepal](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=NP&t=law). A section-by-section site lists "Section 16: Seller's Liability" — [NepalLaws.com](https://nepallaws.com/?p=34584).
- Industry criticism: the Act is described as "a legal milestone or a maze of confusion" with ambiguity on how intermediaries/marketplaces are treated — [B360 Nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/25494/the-e-commerce-act-2081-a-legal-milestone-or-a-maze-of-confusion). Commentators on the draft directive argued it places a significant burden on intermediary platforms and recommended a safe-harbour provision — [ICT Frame recommendations](https://ictframe.com/?p=105413); CAN Federation proposed removing imprisonment in favour of categorised fines — [Technology Khabar](https://www.technologykhabar.com/?p=226916). Whether the final directive adopted these is unconfirmed.

**Electronic Commerce Directive, 2082 (2026)**
- Draft directive published 6 January 2026 — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2026/01/08/nepal-releases-draft-electronic-commerce-directive-2082-to-regulate-online-shopping); final Electronic Commerce Directive, 2082 released Wednesday 18 February 2026 — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/electronic-commerce-directive-2082).
- From January 2026 the Ministry can enforce ICT minimum standards on online business platforms under the "E-commerce Guidelines, 2026" — [Kathmandu Post, 10 Jan 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/01/10/online-business-platforms-come-under-ict-minimum-standards) (likely the same instrument as the Directive; naming differs between outlets).
- Platforms operating without mandatory listing will be cancelled; existing unregistered platforms get 35 days after public notice; a separate guide describes a 7-day cure window after inspection — [CollegeNP, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.collegenp.com/news/unregistered-e-commerce-platforms-face-cancellation); [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/strict-regulation-on-e-commerce-mandatory-registration-and-cancellation-of-non-compliant-platforms); [Peoples Review](https://peoplesreview.com.np/2026/01/08/govt-tightens-rules-on-online-payments-and-e-commerce-registration/).

**Consumer Protection Act, 2075 (2018)**
- Consumers have a "right to return" under Section 14 and can seek refunds/exchanges within 7 days — [Nepal Legal Firm consumer refund](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/consumer-protection-refund-laws-nepal) (secondary; statute not read).

### Inferences
- A services marketplace (photographers bidding on jobs) squarely falls within "sale or delivery of services within Nepal using digital means", so the DoC listing is mandatory before launch. Budget ~1 week and minimal fees for listing on top of OCR/PAN.
- Because the Act's intermediary-liability position is contested (no explicit safe harbour reported), the platform should contractually position itself as an intermediary, display seller (photographer) identity, and run a documented complaint/refund process to limit "seller's liability" exposure under Section 16.
- A commission-only platform will usually cross the NPR 30 lakh services VAT threshold once GMV is modest (e.g., 15% take rate on NPR 2 crore GMV = NPR 30 lakh), so plan for VAT registration from roughly year one; VAT registration also lowers withholding on your own receivables (see §2).

### Gaps
- Could not read the Act or Directive text; the exact fine schedule, the DoC listing fee (if any), and how the Directive defines "intermediary" vs "seller" remain unverified.
- No source gave the OCR's official current fee notice; figures are from practitioner tables.

## 2. Taxes on the marketplace: VAT on commission vs gross; DST; TDS when paying photographers (Income Tax Act s.88/89); treatment of freelancer income; IT-export incentives

### Takeaway
Nepal taxes the platform's **commission** at 13% VAT; the only precedent for taxing a platform on gross flows is the July 2026 ride-sharing regime (5% VAT on fares collected by the platform + 13% on commission + 1% TDS on drivers), which shows the IRD is willing to impose gross-flow collection duties on platforms sector-by-sector. When the platform pays Nepali photographers it must withhold TDS under Income Tax Act s.88: **15%** on service fees to a non-VAT-registered person and **1.5%** when the provider is VAT-registered and issues a VAT invoice. Freelancers receiving foreign currency directly via banks face a flat 5% final withholding (Finance Act 2080 onwards, per secondary sources). IT/digital **export** income enjoys a large exemption (50% in 2080/81; 75% in FY 2082/83; reported rollback to 50% in FY 2083/84) — but only if the platform's own revenue counts as exported IT service. All [COST]/[HASSLE], no blockers.

### Cited Findings

**VAT on commission vs gross**
- Standard VAT rate 13%; services registration threshold NPR 30 lakh — [Law Alpine VAT](https://lawalpine.com/blog/vat-in-nepal-rates-and-thresholds-2082-83).
- Ride-sharing precedent (IRD public notice issued 15 July 2026, effective 17 July 2026 / Shrawan 1): platforms must collect **5% VAT on the taxable value of the transport service** (the fare), issue the prescribed invoice on the driver's behalf and remit by the 25th of the following month, with no input credit on that 5%; the platform's **own commission and service fees remain at 13% VAT** — [LookupTax Nepal ride-share VAT 2026](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/nepal/ride-sharing-platform-vat-2026); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/nepal-tax-agency-issues-public-notice-on-vat-obligations-for-rideshare-service-providers). Press example: on a Rs 100 Pathao fare the driver remits Rs 6 (Rs 5 VAT + Rs 1 TDS) via the platform — [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/ride-sharing-to-be-taxed-from-shrawan-1-fares-set-to-rise).
- Non-resident DST (2%) and VAT apply only to foreign suppliers of digital services to Nepali consumers — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2022/11/04/digital-service-tax-dst-and-value-added-tax-vat-registration-for-digital-service-provided-by-non-resident-person).

**Withholding (TDS) on payments to photographers — Income Tax Act 2058, s.88**
- IRD's unofficial English rate table lists the 15% general service-fee rate and a 1.5% rate for "payment of service charge to the VAT registered service provider resident person" under s.88 — [IRD unofficial translation (PDF)](https://www.ird.gov.np/public/pdf/1747549774.pdf).
- A 2026 practitioner note: IRD currently states service fees paid to a VAT-registered resident provider attract 1.5% withholding; the general service-fee rate otherwise is 15% — [Niti Partners](https://nitipartners.com/?p=9872); same split (1.5% with VAT invoice, 15% on PAN bill) — [Law Alpine TDS](https://lawalpine.com/blog/tds-in-nepal); [Pradhan & Associates Income Tax Rates 2081/82 (PDF)](https://pradhanlaw.com/file-upload/files/P&A%20Briefing%20-%20Income%20Tax%20Rates%202081-2082%20(2024-2025%20A.D.)%2018.07.24.pdf).
- Supreme Court precedent: payments to foreign companies with source in Nepal attract 15% under s.88(1) — [Khabarhub](https://english.khabarhub.com/2023/25/319730).
- Ride-share drivers: 1% TDS collected via the platform from Shrawan 1, 2083 — [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/ride-sharing-to-be-taxed-from-shrawan-1-fares-set-to-rise) (a sector-specific concession, not the general s.88 rate).

**Freelancer income from abroad**
- Individuals not running a registered business pay a flat 5% final withholding on foreign-currency payments received through a bank, financial institution or licensed remittance company; the bank withholds at conversion — [Bizsewa freelancer tax](https://bizsewa.com/?p=4673); [Kumari Job freelance tax 2026](https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/general-information/freelance-income-tax-nepal). Legal origin attributed to Finance Act 2080 ("5% advance tax on payments received from abroad for software, multimedia or consultancy work") — [Froxjob](https://froxjob.com/blog/nepal-cuts-income-tax-to-5-for-it-professionals-working-with-foreign-clients).
- Outlier: one guide claims 1% TDS on foreign currency into personal accounts is final — [Rabi-info](https://rabi-info.pages.dev/freelancing-from-nepal); uncorroborated.
- Caveat from a law firm: "freelancers in Nepal pay 5% tax on foreign income" is too broad; income from Nepali clients is taxed under normal slabs — [Nepal Legal Firm freelancer tax](https://nepallegalfirm.pages.dev/articles/freelancer-tax-nepal-2025). Many freelancers do not file and face penalties — [ShareSansar, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/why-so-many-nepali-freelancers-dont-file-tax-and-what-it-costs-them-2026-10-29).

**IT / digital-service export incentives (company level)**
- Economic Bill 2080 (FY 2023/24) introduced a 50% income-tax discount for five years for companies providing IT-based BPO, software, cloud and similar services from Nepal — [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/blog/government-offers-50-tax-break-on-it-service-exports-to-boost-sector); [Headline Nepal](https://english.headlinenepal.com/details/32885).
- Budget FY 2082/83 (May 2025) raised the IT-service-export exemption to 75% — [Kathmandu Post, 30 May 2025](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/05/30/budget-2025-26-introduces-sweeping-tax-incentives-for-tech-exports-startups-and-green-hydrogen-firms-while-raising-duties-on-alcohol-tobacco-and-luxury-goods); [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=80552); reported as an effective 5% final rate for IT exporters — [NEPSE Trading FY 2082/83 tax changes](https://nepsetrading.com/blog/major-tax-changes-in-nepal-fy-208283-budget).
- Budget FY 2083/84 (May 2026) reportedly rolled the exemption back from 75% to 50% — [Law Alpine Budget 2083/84](https://lawalpine.com/blog/budget-2083-84-tax-changes-in-nepal) (single source; unverified).

### Inferences
- For a photographer marketplace, the IRD has (so far) only imposed gross-flow VAT collection on ride-share; absent a sector notice, the platform charges 13% VAT on its commission only, and each photographer is responsible for his/her own VAT (most will be under the NPR 30 lakh threshold and will not charge VAT).
- Consequence of s.88: when the platform disburses NPR to a Nepali photographer who is not VAT-registered, it must withhold **15%** and file monthly e-TDS returns; only VAT-registered photographers get 1.5%. A 15% deduction is a serious supply-side friction (compare India's 0.1% under s.194-O) and the biggest tax issue for the model. Options to test with a tax adviser: (a) structure the platform as an agent so the *client* is the payer (a corporate client is itself a withholding agent; an individual consumer is generally not), or (b) seek a sector-specific concession like ride-share's 1%.
- The 5% final foreign-income regime applies to individuals receiving foreign currency directly; if the platform collects from a foreign client and pays the photographer in NPR, the photographer's receipt is domestic-source service income subject to s.88 withholding, not the 5% regime — this makes "platform in the middle" less tax-efficient for the photographer than direct billing, unless the platform can pass through foreign-currency receipts (see §4).
- Whether the platform's commission on jobs for *foreign* clients counts as "IT service export" eligible for the 50–75% exemption is uncertain; marketplace intermediation is not obviously "software/BPO".

### Gaps
- A "NPR 40 lakh annual cap" on the 5% freelancer regime appeared in search summaries but could not be attributed to a URL; treat as unverified.
- No source addressed whether an online marketplace's intermediation fee could be VAT-exempt or zero-rated when the client is abroad (export of services zero-rating).
- Could not confirm the IRD's current TDS return filing cadence/penalties for e-TDS.

## 3. Payments: can a Nepali startup hold client money in escrow and release it to freelancers? Payment and Settlement Act 2075; PSP/PSO licensing; riding on eSewa/Khalti/Fonepay/ConnectIPS; gateway fees; how local marketplaces handle payouts

### Takeaway
There is **no licence category a startup can realistically obtain** to hold third-party funds: the Payment and Settlement Act, 2075 (2018) and NRB's Licensing Policy 2079 (effective 2 January 2023) recognise only PSPs (wallets) and PSOs (switches/card networks), require a Letter of Intent, cash deposit and infrastructure inspection, and NRB has been revoking rather than issuing licences. Nepal has no "payment aggregator" or marketplace-escrow category, and no reported bank escrow product except the FITTA s.14 tripartite escrow for foreign investors. The practical route used by every Nepali platform (Pathao, Foodmandu, Daraz) is **merchant collection + own-account payout**: collect through eSewa/Khalti/Fonepay/bank gateway into the company's own bank account (T+1 settlement), hold the funds as ordinary company money with contractual "release on delivery" terms, and pay photographers by bank transfer/wallet after TDS. True escrow with legal segregation of client money is a [BLOCKER]; a contractual hold-and-release in the company's own account is a [HASSLE] with counterparty-risk and tax (VAT on gross?) questions. NRB's new Regulatory Sandbox (guidelines June 2026) explicitly allows unlicensed fintechs to test with a partner bank and can relax escrow-related requirements, a possible path.

### Cited Findings

**Licensing framework**
- The Payment and Settlement Act, 2075 is the principal legislation; NRB's current Licensing Policy for Institutions Performing Payment-Related Work, 2079 took effect 2 January 2023 and recognises two licence types: Payment Service Provider (PSP) and Payment System Operator (PSO). Applicants first obtain a Letter of Intent; LOI application fees reported as NPR 100,000 (PSP) and NPR 50,000 (PSO); PSO scope is operating a card network or payment switch — [Pradhan Law summary of Licensing Policy 2079](https://pradhanlaw.com/publications/licensing-policy-for-institutions-that-perform-payment-related-work-2079-2023-ad); [Niti Partners fintech licensing](https://nitipartners.com/fintech-license-approval-in-nepal/).
- Earlier policy (2073) required a cash deposit with NRB, infrastructure inspection, and barred one institution from holding both PSP and PSO licences — [NRB Licensing Policy 2073](https://nrb.org.np/ofg/licensing-policy-for-payment-related-institutions-2073-2).
- NRB inspected 18 licensed PSPs in FY 2022/23 and acted against several — [Kathmandu Post, 12 Jan 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/01/12/nepali-payment-service-providers-grapple-with-operational-challenges-report-says); it revoked FirstPay Technology's licence citing s.13(6) of the Act — [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=95962), and Prabhu Technology's — [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/nrb-revokes-licenses-of-prabhu-technology-and-first-pay-technology). Licensed PSP/PSO lists are published by NRB — [NRB PSD list](https://www.nrb.org.np/psd/licensed-list-of-payment-system-operator-pso-and-payment-service-provider-psp-9/).
- A PSP's settlement account is used to meet payment obligations; transaction amounts must not exceed the balance in the settlement account — [B360 e-wallets and PSP operation](https://b360nepal.com/e-wallets-and-operation-of-payment-service-providers/).
- June 2026: NRB heavily amended its Integrated Directive on Payment Systems (2080), effective immediately under s.45 of the Act, with "strict rules" on PSPs and agents framed around protecting consumer funds — [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=118469); [NEPSE Trading](https://nepsetrading.com/news/nrb-tightens-regulations-for-payment-service-providers-what-it-means-for-consumers-and-agents?lang=en). Specific settlement/trust-account text not obtained.
- Commentary on regulatory gaps: NRB had said firms operating payment gateways were legal but could not be licensed for lack of a framework — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/business/nrb-to-bring-firms-operating-payment-gateway-under-its-ambit); fintech regulatory gaps — [Kathmandu Post column, 14 Mar 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2024/03/14/regulatory-gaps-in-nepal-s-fintech).
- Pending: a draft NRB Act amendment (March 2026) proposes bringing PSOs/PSPs within the definition of "financial institutions" — [Kathmandu Post, 1 Mar 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/03/01/nepal-moves-to-redefine-digital-payments-under-nrb-law-stirring-debate-over-tax-regulatory-overlap); [eKantipur](https://ekantipur.com/business/2026/03/01/en/draft-amendment-to-the-nepal-rastra-bank-act-proposal-to-include-payment-system-operators-and-service-providers-in-financial-institutions-41-52.html).

**Escrow**
- The only statutory escrow found: Foreign Investment and Technology Transfer Act 2075 s.14 lets a *foreign investor* execute a tripartite escrow agreement with a partner and an NRB-recognised commercial bank acting as agent — [NepalLaws FITTA s.14](https://nepallaws.com/Laws/foreign-investment-and-technology-transfer-act-2075/chapter-2-foreign-investment/section-14-agreement-may-be-made-with-commercial-bank/). No general bank escrow product or NRB escrow directive for marketplaces surfaced.
- NRB's Regulatory Sandbox guideline (reported effective 14 May 2026) lets NRB temporarily ease several requirements for participants, the list including escrow-related conditions — [NepalNews](https://english.nepalnews.com/?p=221033). Final "Guidelines on Regulatory Sandbox" issued June 2026; eligible participants include BFIs, PSOs, PSPs and fintech companies, and unlicensed fintechs may participate by partnering with a licensed BFI; a high-level Sandbox Governing Committee oversees it; only activities within NRB's oversight (or supporting regulated activities) are eligible — [Beema Post](https://en.beemapost.com/2026/06/13842/); [Kathmandu Post, 24 Jun 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/06/24/regulatory-sandbox-guidelines-aim-to-foster-nepal-fintech-innovation); draft was August 2025 — [eKantipur](https://ekantipur.com/business/2025/08/03/en/rashtra-banks-regulatory-sandbox-to-regulate-and-promote-innovation-in-fintech-28-19.html). NRB also launched a Digital Finance Innovation Hub in March 2025 — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/03/28/nepal-rastra-bank-launches-digital-finance-innovation-hub-to-boost-fintech).

**Gateway fees and settlement (no official rate cards found)**
- Khalti merchant terms: a percentage service charge on every successful transaction, deducted in real time; one-time API fee at signing; annual account maintenance charge from year two; commercials revisable by mutual consent — [Khalti merchant terms](https://khalti.com/info/terms/merchant/); Khalti charges page — [Khalti charges](https://Khalti.com/info/charges/). Khalti FAQ says no charge to transfer funds to bank.
- eSewa: API access free for approved merchants; percentage commission per successful transaction set in the merchant agreement — [eSewa merchant blog](https://blog.esewa.com.np/interested-in-becoming-an-esewa-merchant-heres-how-you-can-do-it/).
- Third-party estimates: eSewa 1.75–1.95% — [PaymentProviders.io](https://paymentproviders.io/providers/esewa.md); both wallets 1–2% — [WebsNP](https://www.websnp.com/help/esewa-khalti-payment-integration-website); 2–3.5% depending on category/volume — [Merokalam](https://merokalam.pages.dev/blog/online-business-registration-nepal/). Settlement typically T+1 to linked bank for both — [Arjan KC comparison](https://www.arjankc.com.np/blog/esewa-vs-khalti-payment-gateway-nepal). ### Inferences
- Nepal has no equivalent of India's "payment aggregator" or the US "money transmitter" category sitting between merchant and PSP. A marketplace that collects into its *own* merchant account and pays out from its *own* bank account is operating as an ordinary merchant/commission agent, which is how Pathao/Foodmandu/Daraz appear to operate. There is no public evidence NRB has treated this as unlicensed payment business, but it is also unregulated: client money is not segregated and would be an unsecured liability of the company in insolvency.
- The ride-share precedent (platform collects the full fare and remits driver-side VAT/TDS) implies the IRD is comfortable with platforms handling gross flows; it also means collecting gross could invite IRD treating you as the supplier. Document the agency arrangement (client ↔ photographer contract; platform as agent) in T&Cs.
- Split settlement at the gateway level (e.g., Fonepay/NCHL routing part of a payment to the photographer's account) was not found as an available product; payouts will be batch bank transfers (ConnectIPS/NCHL IPS) or wallet transfers, with 15%/1.5% TDS withheld.
- The June-2026 sandbox is the only formal path to test a segregated "hold and release" with a partner bank; expect it to be slow and bank-dependent.

### Gaps
- No text of the Payment and Settlement By-laws 2077 or Integrated Directive 2080 (as amended June 2026) was accessible; whether "holding funds for release to a third party" is an activity reserved to licensees is unconfirmed either way.
- No official eSewa/Khalti/Fonepay/IME Pay/NCHL merchant rate cards found; all percentages are third-party estimates.
- No source on NCHL connectIPS/IPS bulk-payout fees for businesses.

## 4. Receiving money from foreign clients: Stripe/PayPal/Wise status; what Nepali startups actually use; NRB foreign-exchange rules for service exports (FERA 2019 BS, Unified Circulars); caps; IT-export tax incentive

### Takeaway
Nepal remains outside PayPal (cannot receive), Stripe (no native Nepal account) and Wise business, and the Foreign Exchange (Regulation) Act 2019 BS plus NRB's Unified Forex Circulars govern every inbound dollar. What has changed by late 2026: (1) NRB allowed licensed wallets to receive foreign-currency payments for goods/services with settlement in NPR through a Nepali bank (April 2025); (2) **Khalti–Stripe (April 2026)** lets a Nepali business/freelancer accept international cards via a Stripe-hosted checkout into a Khalti wallet at a 2% fee, NPR-converted; (3) NRB's Circular 10/2082/83 (6 April 2026) raised card/forex limits for IT exporters (reported $5,000–$10,000); (4) the Payment and Settlement (First Amendment) Bill, 2083 — which creates a licensing route for *foreign* payment institutions (10 countries/10 years track record) — passed the House of Representatives on 23 Sept 2026 but National Assembly passage/certification is unconfirmed. Inbound service-export receipts are not capped but must land in a Nepali bank/wallet and be converted to NPR (foreign-currency retention is restricted). Receiving money is a [HASSLE]/[COST] (2–4% all-in), not a blocker; *holding* USD and paying it onward abroad is the blocker (see §5).

## 5. Nepal → India: paying Indian photographers; UPI–Fonepay/NCHL–NPCI linkage; INR restrictions and FEMA; whether an Indian subsidiary is required; Press Note 3; Indian GST (s.9(5), s.52 TCS), s.194-O TDS; subsidiary incorporation; DPDP Act

### Takeaway
Expanding the *same Nepali entity* into India is blocked on two fronts. **Outbound:** Nepal's Act Restricting Investment Abroad, 2021 (1964) still prohibits Nepali persons from investing abroad except under gazette/NRB exemptions; NRB's Fifth Amendment to the Foreign Loan and Investment Management By-law (30 Dec 2025) allows only up to about **USD 20,000** outward investment without approval (larger amounts for IT exporters tied to export earnings), so capitalising an Indian subsidiary from Nepal is tightly rationed. **Inbound to India:** Nepal is a land-border country under **Press Note 3 of 2020**, so any Nepali-owned FDI into an Indian company needs prior Government of India approval (inter-ministerial, Home-Ministry-led), except that since **10 March 2026** stakes below 10% can use the automatic route — useless for a controlling subsidiary. **Payment rails:** Fonepay–UPI (live 8 Mar 2024) only lets Indians pay Nepali merchants; the NCHL NPI–UPI link (live 9 Jun 2026) is **P2P remittance** through select banks, not merchant payouts. India–Nepal transactions must be settled in INR under FEMA's Manner of Receipt and Payment Regulations 2023; Nepali cardholders are capped at IRs 100,000/month in India (2018 NRB circular), while bank drafts/TT in INR have historically been uncapped and the two central banks were negotiating an interbank channel for large INR transfers as of Feb 2026. If an Indian entity exists, it must register for GST as an e-commerce operator irrespective of turnover (s.24(x)), collect **TCS at 0.5%** under s.52 on supplies through the platform (photography is not a s.9(5) notified service, so the platform is not the deemed supplier), deduct **0.1% TDS under s.194-O** on payouts (5% without PAN), and comply with the **DPDP Rules 2025** (notified 13/14 Nov 2025; core fiduciary duties from ~13 May 2027). Net: India requires an Indian company with Indian (or non-land-border) capital, or a partnership with an Indian operator — a [BLOCKER] for a Nepal-funded subsidiary, a [HASSLE]+[COST] for a locally funded one.

### Inferences
- **Paying Indian photographers from Nepal without an Indian entity:** legally the payment must be in INR (FEMA) and would be an outbound service-import payment from Nepal needing NRB-permitted channels (INR draft/TT via a Nepali bank; the card route is capped at IRs 100,000/month). Per-job bank drafts to hundreds of freelancers are operationally unworkable; the NPI–UPI link is P2P only. Until an interbank INR channel or a B2B/merchant leg of UPI–NPI launches, Nepal-side payouts to India are a practical [BLOCKER].
- **Indian subsidiary funded from Nepal:** USD 20,000 outward cap + PN3 government approval = de facto blocker for a Nepali founder's wholly owned Indian subsidiary. Workable structures: (a) an Indian company owned by Indian residents/partners with a licensing or revenue-share agreement with the Nepali company (Pioneer Law's "technology transfer" pathway); (b) a non-land-border holding company (e.g., Singapore/US) — but Nepal's outward-investment cap applies to funding that too; (c) partner with an existing Indian platform.
- An Indian entity running the marketplace would be an ECO: register GST in each state of operation (or at least where it has presence), collect 0.5% TCS on photographer supplies (photographers above ₹20 lakh must be GST-registered; the s.24(ix) exemption for small service providers supplying via ECOs would need checking), deduct 0.1% TDS under s.194-O, and build DPDP consent/notice/breach processes before May 2027.
- Nepal–India data flows: the DPDP Act permits cross-border transfer except to restricted countries notified by the Government; no restriction naming Nepal was found, but hosting Indian user data in Nepal should be reviewed once restriction notifications issue.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed whether the Fifth Amendment's USD 20,000 is per company per year or cumulative, or whether IT exporters still enjoy the USD 1 million/50%-of-earnings route alongside it.
- No source on the PN3 approval timeline/success rate for Nepali applicants specifically.
- Whether CBIC's 2018 FAQ position (foreign ECO registers in each state) is still current was not verified.
- No source on an Indian-side business/merchant leg for UPI–NPI (payouts to Indian accounts from Nepali businesses).

## 6. Nepali startup support (Startup Enterprise Loan, National Startup Policy 2080), payment sandbox, and data-protection obligations (Privacy Act 2075, ETA 2063, pending IT & Cybersecurity Bill 2082)

### Takeaway
Support is real but small: the Startup Enterprise Loan (Procedure 2081) lends up to NPR 25 lakh at 3% with the project as collateral, routed through Rastriya Banijya Bank (5,158 applications for a NPR 1 billion pool in FY 2081/82); the National Startup Policy 2080 (2024) promises up to 10-year income-tax exemption and registration-fee waivers, and a March 2025 Industrial Enterprise Regulation amendment finally defined "startup". NRB's Regulatory Sandbox (June 2026) is the only payments sandbox. Data obligations come from the Individual Privacy Act 2075 (consent for collection/processing, duties in ss.23–28), the Electronic Transactions Act 2063 (offences), and the E-Commerce Act 2081 (protect/allow amendment of personal data); a replacement IT & Cybersecurity Bill 2082 (tabled Aug 2025) would add retention/destruction duties. All [HASSLE]/[COST].

### Inferences
- The startup loan (≤ NPR 25 lakh ≈ USD 18k) is seed-scale only; the 10-year tax holiday is valuable but depends on qualifying under the 2025 definition and on IRD implementation.

### Gaps

## 7. What changes for global (US/UK) clients

### Takeaway
Serving US/UK clients from the Nepali entity is the *easiest* cross-border case: inbound payments are allowed (Khalti–Stripe card checkout, bank gateways, SWIFT), FX converts to NPR, and the income is plausibly "IT/digital service export" for the 50–75% exemption. The hard limits are: no native Stripe/PayPal account (so no Stripe Connect-style split payouts), no ability to hold a USD float or pay foreign photographers, and 2–4%+ acceptance cost. The common workaround is a foreign holding/operating company (US LLC/UK Ltd) that runs the global platform on Stripe/Wise — but Nepal's outward-investment cap (USD 20,000 without approval) and the 1964 Act make that a [BLOCKER] for a Nepal-resident founder unless funded by non-Nepali investors or structured via technology-transfer/licensing.

### Cited Findings
- Khalti–Stripe accepts international cards for Nepali users at 2%, NPR-settled — [Khalti FAQ](https://blog.khalti.com/offers/frequently-asked-questions-receive-international-payments-directly-in-your-khalti-wallet-via-stripe/).
- Nepali banks' gateways accept foreign Visa/Mastercard/UnionPay — [ICT Frame Nabil EPG](https://ictframe.com/?p=80105); [ShareSansar NMB](https://www.sharesansar.com/index.php/newsdetail/nmb-bank-launches-secure-online-payment-gateway-to-boost-e-commerce-in-nepal-2025-12-01).
- Native Stripe requires a foreign (e.g., US LLC) entity — [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/how-nepal-based-founders-can-set-up-stripe-for-an-online-business); PayPal cannot receive in Nepal — [Merokalam](https://merokalam.pages.dev/blog/paypal-account-in-nepal/).
- Outward investment limited to ~USD 20,000 without approval (Fifth Amendment, 30 Dec 2025) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/inward-foreign-investment/1727272/nrb-eases-equity-inflows-repatriation-and-outward-investment); IT exporters earlier allowed USD 1m / 50% of FX earnings — [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/?p=82753).
- IT-export income exemption 75% (FY 2082/83), reportedly 50% (FY 2083/84) — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/05/30/budget-2025-26-introduces-sweeping-tax-incentives-for-tech-exports-startups-and-green-hydrogen-firms-while-raising-duties-on-alcohol-tobacco-and-luxury-goods); [Law Alpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/budget-2083-84-tax-changes-in-nepal).
- Foreign payment institutions could be licensed in Nepal if the 2083 amendment bill is enacted (10 countries/10 years test) — [New Business Age](https://newbusinessage.com/news/45347/government-proposes-strict-entry-barriers-for-global-payment-companies-in-nepal); HoR passed 23 Sept 2026 — [MyRepublica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/house-unanimously-passes-payment-and-settlement-first-amendment-bill-62-82.html).

### Inferences
- "Nepal photographers for global clients" works as a Nepal-only supply-side marketplace with foreign demand: collect USD/GBP → NPR, pay Nepali photographers in NPR with 15%/1.5% TDS. VAT: export of services may be zero-rated (unverified); client-side US/UK sales tax/VAT generally not triggered by B2B photography services but check UK VAT place-of-supply if B2C.
- "Global photographers for global clients" is not feasible from a Nepali entity (cannot pay out abroad). Only a foreign entity can do that, which runs into the outward-investment restriction for the founder.

### Gaps
- No source on Nepal VAT zero-rating of exported platform services or on NRB documentation required per inbound SWIFT receipt (invoice/contract thresholds).

## 8. Summary classification: blockers vs hassles vs costs (as of 7 Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Nepal-only launch is feasible within ~4–8 weeks and modest cost; the hard legal walls are (i) no escrow/aggregator licence category, (ii) no outward payments to foreign freelancers, (iii) Nepal's outward-investment ban plus India's Press Note 3 for any India subsidiary, and (iv) no business-grade Nepal→India payout rail.

### Cited Findings
- **[BLOCKER] Holding client funds in a segregated escrow:** only PSP/PSO licences exist; NRB revoking not granting; sandbox is the sole path — [Pradhan Law](https://pradhanlaw.com/publications/licensing-policy-for-institutions-that-perform-payment-related-work-2079-2023-ad); [Beema Post sandbox](https://en.beemapost.com/2026/06/13842/).
- **[BLOCKER] Paying foreign (incl. ### Inferences
- Recommended structure for validation: Nepali Pvt Ltd (authorised capital ≤ NPR 25 lakh → NPR 9,500 OCR fee), PAN + voluntary VAT registration, DoC e-commerce listing, eSewa/Khalti/Fonepay merchant accounts plus Khalti–Stripe for foreign clients, payouts by ConnectIPS/wallet after TDS, T&Cs framing the platform as agent with a contractual "release on delivery" hold in the company's own account. Defer India until either (a) an Indian partner entity exists or (b) the NRB–RBI interbank INR channel and a business leg of UPI–NPI go live.
