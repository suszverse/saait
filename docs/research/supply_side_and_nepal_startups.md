# Supply side of a Nepali photographer / content-creator marketplace, and the Nepali consumer-internet track record

Research date: 2026-11-07. Method note: web search only. The session's network allowlist blocked direct fetches of kathmandupost.com, english.onlinekhabar.com, nextbillion.net, katmandujournal.com, praxiumlabs.com and teamventures.com.np, so findings from those outlets are taken from search-result extracts, not full articles; treat quoted figures from them as needing a second look. Items older than 2023 are flagged "(older)".

## KQ1. Who the Nepali photographers / videographers / editors / creators are: counts, income, full-time vs side-gig, gear vs income, seasonality, client acquisition, use of Upwork/Fiverr

### Takeaway
There is no census of Nepali photographers; the only hard proxies are community sizes (the "Nepalese in Photography" Facebook group reports ~293,800 members) and directory listings (a few hundred studios). Income is lumpy and seasonal: client-facing wedding packages run roughly NPR 15,000–300,000, freelancer per-event fees are reported around NPR 10,000–50,000, staff photographers earn NPR 25,000–45,000/month, and a camera body alone costs NPR 76,000–370,000 with a 2–3 year upgrade cycle. Work concentrates in the saait (auspicious-date) wedding months; off-season is spent editing. Client acquisition today is Instagram DMs, phone/referrals and wedding directories, not marketplaces.

- Nepal Yellow Pages lists 78 photography studios, 28 photographers and 313 "photography" listings — [NepalYP studios](https://www.nepalyp.com/category/Studios); [NepalYP photographers](https://www.nepalyp.com/category/Photographers). Directory counts, not a census.

Seasonality and off-season behaviour

Late Falgun is the most heavily booked window; studios advise booking 3–6 months ahead and say slots "fill up very quickly in peak months" — [Wedding Story best photographers](https://weddingstory.com.np/best-wedding-photographer-nepal).

- Upwork's Nepal video-editor pages list Kathmandu editors at $30/hr (Premiere/After Effects) including "a wedding-film editor with 100+ projects for clients in the UK, US, Canada and Europe", alongside others at $6–10/hr doing YouTube/faceless-channel edits — [Upwork video editors Nepal](https://www.upwork.com/hire/video-editors/np/); [Upwork Kathmandu](https://www.upwork.com/hire/video-editors/np/kathmandu/); [Upwork videographers Nepal](https://www.upwork.com/hire/videographers/np/).
- A Nepal training provider claims Nepali editors win international clients on Upwork/Fiverr at competitive rates (promotional) — [Saarathi Academy](https://saarathiacademy.com.np/blog/why-video-editing-training-in-nepal-is-a-smart-investment).
- Macro context: IIDS study estimates Nepal exported ~USD 515M (Rs 67.93bn) of IT/ITeS services in 2022, +64.2% on 2021 — [OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-exported-it-services.html); it cites ~66,509 freelancers engaged in exporting IT/ITeS (one breakdown: ~14,728 IT + ~51,781 ITeS) earning on average USD 4,723/year; sample was 86 companies and 389 freelancers — [IIDS "Unleashing IT"](https://iids.org.np/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/unleashing.pdf); [IIDS study page](https://iids.org.np/it-industry-growth-study/). ### Inferences
- A gross of NPR 20,000–100,000 per single-day wedding, with maybe 40 saait days a year and up to several events per peak day, means a working Kathmandu wedding photographer's revenue is front-loaded into ~5 Nepali months; a camera body costing 1–4 months of staff salary and replaced every 2–3 years means gear is 10–25% of a mid-earner's annual gross. Off-season demand (events, corporate, product, reels) is the real gap a marketplace could fill, and editing is the existing off-season activity.
- The creative labour pool is large in enthusiast terms (hundreds of thousands in Facebook groups) but the professional core is probably in the low thousands in the Valley; NIP's size suggests recruitment of hobbyists/side-giggers would be easy, retaining professionals less so.
- Nepali video editors already sell internationally on Upwork at $6–30/hr, so a bidding/marketplace model is not alien to the editor segment; shoot-based photographers are far less exposed to it.

### Gaps
- No full-time vs side-gig split, no survey of photographer income, no national count of photographers. Suggest: survey NIP members directly; ask Kathmandu Photographer Association for membership.
- No reliable per-day wedding volume for Kathmandu; saait-day count is the best proxy.
- Could not open the OnlineKhabar/Kathmandu Post photographer features in full (network block); only extracted quotes.

## KQ2. Freelancer attitudes to bidding, commissions, delayed payouts and off-platform dealing; what local gig platforms charge and how supply reacted

### Takeaway
Hard evidence on Nepali freelancers' feelings about bidding is thin (no Nepal-specific survey or Reddit thread surfaced), but the gig-platform record is clear: Pathao's 20% commission triggered a December 2020 rider protest demanding 8%, office vandalism and 26 arrests, and multi-platform driver protests recurred in March and July 2026; inDrive entered at 10%. Payment friction (no PayPal withdrawal, Payoneer ~2%, 5% tax deduction on forex earnings) is the main documented complaint for cross-border freelancers.

### Cited Findings
Local platform commissions
- Pathao Nepal: 20% on bike rides, 15% on car rides; Pathao said 20% "matches international standards" (Lyft, Gojek, Ola, Grab) — [myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/commission-rate-fixed-at-20-as-per-int-l-standard-pathao); a comparison blog describes a sliding scale (1–5 rides/day 20%, 6–9 15%, 10–14 10%, 15–19 5%, 20+ 1%), single-source and unofficial — [RydNepal](https://www.rydnepal.com/blog/pathao-vs-indrive-vs-yango-rider-earnings). Rider gross earnings cited at Rs 1,000–3,500/day, net ~Rs 700–1,700 after commission, fuel, rent — [Kumarijob](https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/job-salary-in-nepal/pathao-rider-salary-in-nepal-2026); [RydNepal gig guide](https://www.rydnepal.com/blog/gig-economy-kathmandu-bike-rental).
- inDrive launched officially in Nepal May 2024 taking 10% commission from drivers (incl. taxes per one outlet) — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/05/07/indrive-launches-officially-in-nepal); [TechLekh](https://techlekh.com/indrive-commission-nepal/).
- Foodmandu merchant commission ~20–22% (highest among platforms; Foodbusters 14–15%, Pathao Food 18%) — [The Record (older, ~2021)](https://www.recordnepal.com/the-business-of-delivering-food); [Gurkha Tech case study](https://gurkhatech.com/foodmandu-case-study-nepal-ecommerce-pioneer-2/).
- Daraz Nepal seller commission by category typically 6–15% plus 2.25% payment fee and a handling fee (schedule effective 1 June 2026) — [Arjan KC Daraz guide](https://arjankc.com.np/blog/daraz-seller-guide-nepal-2026/); [Pakhisaab](https://pakhisaab.com/guides/selling-on-daraz-fees-and-profit/).

Supply reaction
- Dec 2020 (older): Pathao riders demanded commission cut from 20% to 8%, insurance, no suspension without notice (11-point demand on Dec 21); protest at Baneshwor office turned violent; 26 arrested; Pathao said it could not reduce commission citing server, navigation, insurance and bonus costs — [OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/pathao-riders-prepare-for-protest-against-the-company.html); [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/discontented-pathao-riders-vandalise-companys-office); [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/pathao-nepal-protest-reason-of-dispute/); [TechLekh](https://techlekh.com/pathao-riders-vandalize-office/).
- Mar 2026: Independent Drivers Foundation Nepal protested Pathao, inDrive and Yango over high commissions, insurance non-implementation and fares vs fuel prices — [Nepal Views](https://english.nepalviews.com/2026/03/30/independent-riders-staging-protest-photos). Jul 2026: drivers protested again demanding lower commissions and fairer fares, noting they bear the new 5% VAT introduced at the start of the fiscal year — [Khabarhub](https://english.khabarhub.com/2026/07/562256/).
- Pathao scale for context: >190,000 registered bike riders, >10,000 taxi drivers, >5,000 food-delivery registrants (~Sept 2026) — [myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/pathao-expands-bike-ride-service-to-17-more-cities-in-nepal-rider-registration-underway); ~35,000 active bike riders and ~15,000 rides/day, 500,000 app downloads (~Oct 2026) — [myRepublica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/how-a-startup-became-nepal-s-leading-ride-hailing-firm). Earlier: 75,000 registered riders, 1.2M customers — [The Record (older)](https://www.recordnepal.com/taken-for-a-ride-by-pathao).
- I found no 2024–2026 Foodmandu rider or restaurant commission protest.

Cross-border freelancer pain points
- (older, 2020) Kathmandu Post: PayPal demands international card/banking details many Nepalis lack; some clients default after delivery, one freelancer "spending more time following up on payment than working"; another said Fiverr/Upwork payments were never delayed; time zones and language sometimes bigger issues than payment — [Kathmandu Post 2020](https://kathmandupost.com/science-technology/2020/08/11/how-do-digital-freelancers-fare-in-nepal).
- Payoneer is "the most widely used international payment service among Nepali freelancers"; withdrawal to NPR in 1–3 business days minus ~2% conversion; cannot be funded from Nepal (NRB outward restrictions) — [ForexFee guide](https://www.forexfee.com/guides/receive-international-payments-nepal). PayPal: can open but cannot withdraw to a Nepali bank — [Merokalam](https://merokalam.com/blog/paypal-account-in-nepal/). Wise support for Nepal is contested between sources — [Remotify](https://www.remotify.co/how-nepali-freelancers-can-receive-usd-and-eur/); [PayoutMap](https://payoutmap.com/country/nepal). SWIFT 3–7 days with multiple fee deductions — [Payxem](https://payxem.com/blog/how-freelancers-in-nepal-get-paid/).
- Banks deduct 5% tax on creators' foreign-currency (AdSense) earnings — [Reffero](https://reffero.com/blog/how-nepali-creators-make-money); foreign freelance income is taxable and should be declared with a PAN — [Digital Solution Nepal](https://digitalsolutionnepal.com/receive-international-payment-nepal/).
- Global (not Nepal-specific) complaints: Upwork Connects "generates more complaints about Upwork than almost anything else"; some bid 150 connects on one contract — [Ruul](https://ruul.io/blog/is-upwork-legit); [Substack comments](https://ameikpe.substack.com/p/freelancing-in-2025/comments). A Nepali developer blog argues $3/hr bids signal low quality and recommends ≥$10/hr — [Kokil Upwork tips](https://kokil.com.np/blog/upwork-profile-tips-nepal-developers).

### Inferences
- Nepali gig supply has shown it will organise and escalate against a 20% take rate, and the market has since been anchored lower by inDrive's 10%. A photographer marketplace charging Fiverr-style 20% would be above every local reference point; 10–15% (or a client-side fee) is the defensible band, and the 5% VAT on platform services now lands on top of whatever is charged.
- Because ride-hailing riders are price-takers on fares while photographers set their own quotes, a bidding model shifts the "race to the bottom" complaint from the platform to peers; the Kathmandu Post newcomer piece suggests undercutting pressure already exists in the Valley.
- Payout speed is a differentiator: domestic eSewa/Khalti/bank settlement in NPR avoids the Payoneer/PayPal friction that defines cross-border freelancing, but a platform that holds funds in escrow for days will be compared unfavourably to cash-on-the-day wedding norms.

### Gaps
- No Nepal-specific survey, Reddit thread or Facebook-group discussion on bidding/commission attitudes surfaced; r/Nepal searches returned nothing usable. Founder should run a short survey in NIP or interview 10–15 working photographers.
- No evidence either way on off-platform leakage in Nepal; the "DM to book" norm implies it will be the default.

## KQ3. Nepal startup track record: graveyard and survivors (consumer internet, marketplaces, e-commerce, ride-hailing)

### Takeaway
Nepal's consumer-internet graveyard is dominated by general e-commerce (Sastodeal closed 2024 with ~200 jobs lost despite a $1M Dolma cheque and Flipkart/Myntra tie-up; Gyapu sold to BG Holdings in 2022 then went inactive; Kathmandu Post in Aug 2026 also lists Metro Tarkari and Foodmario as inactive) and by regulatory limbo (Tootle: seven years of ride-hailing illegality, a hiatus, relaunch under new owners, and founder arrested in Jan 2024 over an investor dispute). Survivors are the ones with durable unit economics and patient local capital (Foodmandu since 2010, three investor rounds and two partial exits; Pathao Nepal; Upaya logistics raising Rs 280M Series C in Aug 2025) or fintech rails (eSewa, Khalti–IME Pay merger approved June 2025).

### Cited Findings
Graveyard / distressed
- Sastodeal (founded 2011) closed in 2024 with about 200 jobs lost; co-founder Amun Thapa blamed falling consumer demand; Dolma Impact Fund had invested $1M in 2020 and the company had an exclusive cross-border partnership with Myntra and Flipkart — [Kathmandu Post Apr 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/04/14/back-to-physical-stores-e-commerce-falters-in-nepal-s-topsy-turvy-business-climate); explainer: "started small in 2011, grew big, but lost focus" — [Nepali Telecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/sastodeal-shut-down).
- Gyapu Marketplace (founded 2020 in lockdown) was acquired by BG Holdings in 2022 and founder-CEO Gyanendra Khadka left — [ICT Frame](https://ictframe.com/bg-holdings-acquires-gyapu-marketplace/); Crunchbase marks it "permanently closed", Gadgetbyte "temporarily closed" — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/gyapu-marketplace); [Gadgetbyte](https://www.gadgetbytenepal.com/category/gyapu/). A 2026 guide attributes decline to fulfilment bottlenecks and merchant payment grievances (uncited) — [TopNepali](https://topnepali.com/top-nepali-online-shopping-sites).
- Kathmandu Post (Aug 2026) lists Sastodeal, Gyapu, Metro Tarkari and Foodmario among domestic platforms that became inactive — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/08/27/fast-delivery-changes-the-game-for-nepal-s-online-sellers).
- Kaymu Nepal (launched 2013/14) was absorbed when Daraz Group acquired Kaymu in July 2016; Alibaba bought Daraz in May 2018 — [Nepal Database](https://www.nepaldatabase.com/evolution-of-e-commerce-and-daraz-in-nepal). NepBay (2007) was rebranded Thulo in 2019 — [Suman Gaudel](https://sumangaudel.com/shopping-sites-online-stores-nepal/). No closure evidence found for Meroshopping.

Daraz itself announced regional layoffs in Feb 2024 and ~30 Nepal staff were reported laid off in June 2026 amid MD Aanchal Kunwar's departure (company denies "layoffs"); a former employee claims Nepal EBITDA turned positive (unverified) — [Kathmandu Post Jun 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/06/07/daraz-nepal-trims-workforce-amid-south-asia-restructuring); [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/business/412839/daraz-refutes-shutdown-rumors-amid-internal).
- Tootle: government treated ride-sharing as illegal under the Transport Management Act; Patan High Court ordered regulation in Feb 2020; Industrial Enterprises Act amended 1 Feb 2024 to recognise ride-hailing; guidelines still pending Dec 2024 — [OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/tootle-pathao-controversy-5-years-on-nepal-undecided-between-legalising-ride-sharing-and-banning.html); [Kathmandu Post Feb 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/02/06/after-seven-years-of-operation-nepal-aims-to-legalise-ride-sharing-services); [Kathmandu Post Dec 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2024/12/03/ride-hailing-services-stuck-in-legal-limbo-as-government-delays-guidelines). Founder Sixit Bhatta was arrested in Jan 2024 after a business partner's complaint (reported as breach of trust/fraud; police: both invested but "Bhatta alone took the returns"), released on bail 7 Jan 2024 — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/01/05/tootle-ceo-sixit-bhatta-arrested); [Setopati](https://en.setopati.com/social/162369).
- Broader: "nearly 99 percent of startups shut down within a year in the absence of a proper ecosystem" (industry figure's estimate, unverified) and lenders tightened after startups "took loans and vanished" — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/in-nepal-startups-came-took-loans-and-vanished-now-borrowing-isnt-easy/). ## KQ6. Funding environment: round sizes, investors, FITTA and foreign capital, incorporating abroad

### Takeaway
Nepali rounds are small: pre-seed ~NPR 3–10M and seed NPR 10–50M per consultancy benchmarks, Business Oxygen writes ~Rs 5M cheques, Nepal Angel Network deals are $50–120k, and even Dolma (Fund I $36.6M across ~8 companies; Fund II ~$72–75M) writes Series B/C cheques of $1–4M. Foreign investment needs NPR 20M minimum per investor (no minimum for IT via automatic route), repatriation approval was only delegated to A-class banks in Dec 2025, convertible notes are not clearly recognised, and the 1964 Act Restricting Investment Abroad bars Nepali residents from outbound investment, which is why the globally funded "Nepali" companies are foreign-incorporated. Government concessional loans (max NPR 2M, Rs 730M budget FY2025/26) and the NRB innovation-financing window (July 2025) are the realistic non-dilutive sources.

### Cited Findings
Round sizes and investors
- Realistic pre-seed NPR 30 lakh–1 crore; seed NPR 1–5 crore; regional VCs from India, Singapore, Bangkok sometimes participate (consultancy blog) — [Praxium Labs](https://praxiumlabs.com/blog/nepali-startup-funding-2026/).
- Dolma Impact Fund I closed ~$36.6M and invested ~$24M in 8 companies; Fund II second close >$50M, hard cap $75M — [Dolma Fund I 2023 report](https://dolmaimpact.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/DIF-I-Annual-Report-2023.pdf); [US Embassy Nepal](https://np.usembassy.gov/us-development-finance-corporation-joins-nepal-focused-dolma-impact-fund-ii/); final close reported as ~$72M or $75M (late 2024) — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/private-equity/dolma-impact-fund/__63god8cOYTlZX44xOLByOKa4y5SveXEoQ9-jd9dKULU); [Zoom Investors](https://zoominvestors.com/blog/private-equity-in-nepal). Lessons: few foreign LPs believed in Nepal risk-adjusted returns; portfolio companies unprepared for governance/ESG; no sovereign credit rating — [NextBillion](https://nextbillion.net/lessons-from-investing-frontier-market-impact-investors-learn-from-nepal-first-international-private-equity-fund/); [Environmental Finance](https://www.environmental-finance.com/content/analysis/impact-investing-in-a-frontier-market.html).

FITTA and foreign capital
- Minimum foreign investment lowered from NPR 50M to NPR 20M (~USD 150k) per investor via Gazette under FITTA s.3; no minimum for eligible IT industries via the automatic route (one source dates this to late 2023) — [Common Law Nepal](https://commonlaw.com.np/publications/fitta-regulations-nepal); [Digital Consulting Ventures](https://digitalconsultingventures.com/insights/foreign-direct-investment-and-company-incorporation-in-nepal-whats-allowed); [Lawaxion](https://lawaxion.com/foreign-investment-compliance-in-nepal/).
- Convertible notes: legal system "did not clearly recognise them as a form of foreign investment"; NRB refused to approve a convertible debt instrument — [B360](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/10278/graduating-from-ldc-to-an-emerging-investment-hub-challenges-and-reforms-for-nepal); [Katmandu Journal May 2026](https://katmandujournal.com/2026/05/30/nepal-rewrites-business-rules-in-pursuit-of-growth-and-foreign-capital/).
- Repatriation: Dec 2025 NRB amendment delegated approval to A-class banks for most categories; share-sale exits still need two Department of Industry approvals plus NRB; US State Dept (Sept 2026) says repatriation remains "difficult, time consuming, and not guaranteed" — [Niti Partners](https://nitipartners.com/nepal-investment-law-amendment-2025/); [US State Dept 2026](https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/701264_2026-Nepal-Investment-Climate-Statement.pdf).
- Act Restricting Investment Abroad (1964) prohibits outbound investment by Nepalis — [US State Dept 2024](https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2024-investment-climate-statements/nepal/); 55 Nepali business elites invested offshore in defiance of it — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/15427/). ESOP participation in foreign parents' schemes allowed under the Foreign Exchange (Regulation) Act 2019 for employees of Nepal-incorporated subsidiaries, with gaps for remote workers of foreign firms — [Law Bhandari](https://lawbhandari.com/publication/employee-stock-ownership-plans-esop-law-in-nepal-key-highlights).
- Cautionary case: Sagoon Inc (Delaware) raised crores from Nepalis abroad and is near bankruptcy, with allegations of founder control — [CIJ Nepal](https://www.cijnepal.org/sagoon-puts-crores-of-investment-by-nepalis-at-risk).
- CG Corp teamed with investors on a $100M VC fund (announced; deployment unclear) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/nepal-cg-corp-fund-startups/).

### Inferences
- A Nepal-domiciled marketplace can realistically raise NPR 5–50M locally (angels, Business Oxygen, TNA, concessional loans) and would need to reach Series B-like traction before Dolma-type cheques; Indian/global VC into a Nepali Pvt Ltd faces the NPR 20M floor per investor (IT exemption may apply), no SAFE/convertible path, and exit-repatriation friction, so foreign VCs will push for an offshore holdco.
- A Nepali-resident founder cannot legally own a Singapore/Delaware holdco without an exemption, which is why the foreign-incorporated winners had founders already abroad (Uprety, Maskey, Sears, Rokaya/Mishra). A Nepal-resident founder's workable route is a Nepal opco funded locally, with any offshore structure requiring NRB/DoI approvals or a non-resident co-founder.

### Gaps
- No primary Gazette text confirming the NPR 20M threshold date or the IT exemption; law-firm blogs only.
- No verified list of 2025–26 Nepali seed rounds with amounts beyond Upaya, Lamina Labs and NAN's three deals.
