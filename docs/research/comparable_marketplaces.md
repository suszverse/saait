# Comparable on-demand photographer / creative-freelancer marketplaces (global + South Asia) and what their track record says about a local, bid-based photographer marketplace

Research date: 2026-11-07. Method note: direct page fetches were blocked by the session's network policy for every external host tried (frenchtechjournal.com, axios.com, sacra.com, wikipedia.org, petapixel.com, inc42.com, frenchweb.fr, outlookindia.com, leadcapture.io, venturebeat.com, shoott.com, substack.com). All findings below therefore come from search-result snippets of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Figures that conflicted across snippets are flagged. Anything older than 2024 is dated in-line.

Model vocabulary used throughout:
- **Open bidding / reverse auction**: client posts a job, suppliers bid, client (or an algorithm) picks, often on price. (Freelancer.com, Airtasker, pre-2014 TaskRabbit, the model being validated.)
- **Fixed-menu managed marketplace**: platform sets the price, assigns/curates the photographer, often handles editing and delivery. (Snappr, Meero, Perfocal, Splento, Kodakit, Shoott, Flytographer.)
- **Lead-gen**: pros pay per lead or per subscription for visibility; the platform does not price or fulfil the job. (Thumbtack, Bark, WedMeGood, WeddingWire/The Knot, ShaadiSaga, Shadiyana.)

## Key Question 1: Global on-demand photography marketplaces — model, pricing/take rate, traction, outcome

### Takeaway
Every well-funded global on-demand photography company (Meero, Snappr, Kodakit, Splento, Perfocal, Shoott, Flytographer) used a **fixed-price, platform-assigned, managed** model, not open bidding; none used cheapest-bid-wins. The two best-funded ones diverged: Meero ($230M+ raised) shut its photographer marketplace in Feb 2023 and became an AI-visuals SaaS (Diffusely), while Snappr survived by shifting toward enterprise accounts with 15%/9%/0% client-side fees plus a historically 20–35% photographer-side commission. Kodak's Kodakit shut after ~2 years. The consumer-only survivors (Shoott, Flytographer) run narrow, high-frequency-location niches (free mini-sessions sold per photo; travel portraits).

- Twenty20 raised $8M (2015) — [TechCrunch, 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/02/26/twenty20/amp/); acquired by Envato (2019); Envato shut it 31 July 2022 — [Pickr, 2022](https://pickr.com.au/news/2022/envato-ends-its-twenty20-unlimited-photo-service); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_Stock_Photos)

**A small bootstrapped comparable (model for Nepal-scale economics)**
- A 10-year-old photographer booking platform listed for sale on Flippa: 1,200 photographers, ~800 bookings, 30–40% margins, ~$1.9k monthly profit — [Flippa listing](https://flippa.com/11905485-profitable-10-yr-online-platform-with-30-40-margins-1-9k-monthly-profit-1-200-photographers-800-bookings-and-strong-seo-growth-potential) (seller-provided numbers; indicates how small a "working" niche photo marketplace can be)

### Inferences
- The companies that raised the most money (Meero, Snappr) concluded that consumer/SMB one-off shoots do not carry the business; both re-anchored on repeat B2B volume (Meero on fashion/auto SaaS, Snappr on enterprise with 30+ shoots/month). Repeat demand, not matching, was the scarce asset.
- Low photographer pay is the consistent supply-side failure mode of fixed-price managed platforms (Meero €30/hr real estate, Snappr 20–35% cut and "below minimum wage" complaints, Kodakit copyright grab). Platforms that advertise being cheapest to clients end up extracting from photographers.
- No notable global photographer marketplace used open bidding; the market "voted" for fixed menus. The one that lets clients win on price (Snappr's comparison tool) still sets the price itself rather than letting photographers underbid each other.
- Shoott/Flytographer survive because they changed the unit (per photo, not per shoot; travel moments, not weddings) and guarantee photographer floors ($100/hr), which is the opposite of a reverse auction.

### Gaps
- Could not read Meero's exact GMV, photographer count, or Diffusely's current headcount (full articles blocked).
- Snappr's current photographer-side commission (post-2019) and photographer count are unpublished; only the client-side 15%/9%/0% fee is public.
- Flytographer's photographer commission percentage is on a page that did not render in snippets.
- Lemonade was in the brief but appears to be an insurer, not a photography marketplace; no photography company by that name was found.

## Key Question 2: Lead-gen and general services marketplaces (Thumbtack, Bark, TaskRabbit, Airtasker, Freelancer/Upwork/Fiverr) — model, fees, outcomes

### Takeaway
Lead-gen platforms (Thumbtack, Bark) monetize photographers' uncertainty: pros pay $20–90 per lead whether or not they win, several pros buy the same lead, and photographer complaints center on cost-per-booked-job exceeding margin on sub-$300 shoots. TaskRabbit is the clearest documented case of a bidding marketplace abandoning bidding (July 2014) because completion rates fell even as both sides grew; it moved to fixed hourly rates with a 100%-to-tasker payout and client-side fees. Airtasker still runs open bidding with a ~14.5% supplier-side fee. Fiverr (fixed menu, 20% flat) and Upwork (bids + sliding fee) outgrew Freelancer.com (bids, 10%).

### Cited Findings

**Thumbtack (US lead-gen, pay-per-lead)**
- Photographer lead prices $20–75 per lead; high lead costs and low close rates are the common complaints — [LeadCapture, 2023](https://leadcapture.io/blog/thumbtack-lead-cost/)
- Several pros buy the same lead; photographers pay regardless of who the customer chooses — [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/thumbtack/); a photographer on Thumbtack's own forum: up to $90 per lead but only booking jobs of max $250 — [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/6255); pros complaining of "expensive leads but no bookings" and "discounted lead" upsell emails — [Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/7939/); photographers' open letter to Thumbtack — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/apps/open-letter-thumbtack-289019)
- BBB: 1,035 complaints in 3 years, not BBB accredited — [BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/internet-service/thumbtack-inc-1116-367066/complaints?page=100)
- Thumbtack was the only one of 17 marketplaces in Lenny Rachitsky's research that did not constrain supply, because its transactions were low-frequency and high-value, so it "had to cast a wide net" — [kjlabuz summary of Rachitsky](https://kjlabuz.substack.com/p/marketplaceliquidity?s=w) (secondary summary; original Lenny's Newsletter text not retrieved)

**Bark (UK/US lead-gen, credits)**
- Credits cost $2.35 each in the US (£1.80 UK), cheaper in larger packs; each lead's credit cost is shown before responding and varies by job size — [Bark help center](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-us/articles/13346288068892); leads need 3 to 20+ credits — [Medium guide, 2024](https://medium.com/@kcelestin375/how-much-do-bark-leads-cost-2023-update-278b2769efb1); most leads $20–70 to unlock — [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/bark/); credits bought from 1 Nov 2025 expire after 3 months, unrefundable — [Bark help center](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-gb/articles/13346288068892)
- Photographer anecdotes: $24 to contact a lead with no way to ask questions first; "overpriced"; corporate headshot jobs get 4–5 photographers responding at mid-$20s each; one photographer says they've "made more from Bark than spent"; others call it a scam with "clueless" clients — [DPReview forum](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4473162). A UK estimate puts photographers' cost per won job at £250+ — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/) (third-party estimate)

**TaskRabbit (US) — abandoned open bidding in 2014**
- June 2014: TaskRabbit found that while both sides were growing, its task-completion rate was dropping; diagnosis: inefficient matching of supply and demand — [TechCrunch, June 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/17/following-a-drop-in-completed-jobs-errands-marketplace-taskrabbit-shakes-up-its-business-model)
- New direct-hire/fixed-hourly model released 10 July 2014; significant tasker backlash — [VentureBeat, 2014](https://venturebeat.com/business/taskrabbit-pivots-launches-on-demand-service-for-cleaning-handiwork-moving-errands); [RSM Digital Strategy, "TaskRabbit's Fall from Auctioneering Grace"](https://digitalstrategy.rsm.nl/2017/10/21/taskrabbits-fall-from-auctioneering-grace/)
- Fixed rates create "an effective floor on the dollar value of a given task" and avoid a "race-to-the-bottom" — [Harvard D^3 case](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/submission/taskrabbit-your-retinue-of-errand-runners); the 2014 move to fixed pricing "finally brought utilization high enough that their margins could recover" — [BIA](https://www.bia.com/blog/how-taskrabbit-defied-on-demand-logic-to-reach-breakeven/); CEO Leah Busque: 2014 "was really a year of rebuilding… I wish we had done this sooner" — [TechNetwork blog, 2015](https://technetworkblog.blogspot.com/2015/01/through-fire-what-taskrabbit-learned.html)
- Current fee structure: taskers get 100% of their hourly rate and tips; fees are charged to clients (third parties estimate ~22.5% client-side: 15% service + 7.5% trust & support); $25 one-time tasker registration — [TaskRabbit blog](https://www.taskrabbit.com/blog/how-pricing-works-on-taskrabbit-and-what-it-means-for-your-earnings/); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-much-does-taskrabbit-take/)

**Airtasker (Australia) — still open bidding**
- Posters describe a task and suggest a budget; the final price emerges from competing bids — [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/create/how-to-build-website-like-airtasker/); US connection fee 14.5% of task value (min $4.90, cap $49.50) per Airtasker Support, as reported by a third party — [TheWearify](https://thewearify.com/airtasker-vs-taskrabbit/) (unverified against Airtasker's site)

**Freelancer.com vs Upwork vs Fiverr — bidding vs fixed menu**
- Freelancer.com: 10% or $5 (whichever greater) on fixed-price projects, 10% on contests, 15% on some "Preferred" high-value gigs; outbound bidding model, lower-budget/entry-level client mix; client-side 3% — [Jobbers, 2025](https://www.jobbers.io/upwork-vs-fiverr-vs-freelancer-com-vs-zero-commission-platforms-complete-2025-comparison/); [Upwork resources](https://www.upwork.com/resources/fiverr-vs-freelancer)
- Upwork: bidding costs "Connects" (~$0.15 each) on top of a service fee (sources conflict: sliding 20/10/5% vs. 0–15% variable); client 5% — [Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/upwork-vs-fiverr-vs-freelancer-com-vs-zero-commission-platforms-complete-2025-comparison/); [Sidestackers, Jan 2026](https://www.sidestackers.com/blog/upwork-fiverr-freelancer-comparison)
- Fiverr: flat 20% seller fee, fixed-price gigs only, no bidding cost; client 5.5% + $2.95 — [Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/upwork-vs-fiverr-vs-freelancer-com-vs-zero-commission-platforms-complete-2025-comparison/)
- Upwork mitigates auction shortcomings with seller ratings and Top Rated programs — [RSM Digital Strategy, 2016](https://digitalstrategy.rsm.nl/2016/10/07/technology-of-the-week-markets-auctions-and-the-gig-economy/)

### Inferences
- Lead-gen is the model that most reliably monetizes small local service markets (it needs no pricing control, no fulfilment), but it pushes the entire risk onto photographers, and in a low-ticket, low-income market the $20–90/lead arithmetic breaks immediately (Thumbtack photographers already complain at $250 jobs).
- TaskRabbit is the single most relevant precedent for a bid-based local services marketplace: bidding produced falling completion rates despite growth on both sides, and the fix was fixed prices with a floor. The "race to the bottom" was named explicitly by case writers as the thing fixed pricing solved.
- Airtasker shows open bidding can persist, but in a high-income market with a generic task mix and a ~14.5% fee; it is not a photography or wedding specialist.

### Gaps
- No primary data on Thumbtack's photography-category revenue or close rates; everything is pro-reported.
- Could not retrieve TaskRabbit's actual completion-rate figures before/after the 2014 change.
- Airtasker's current fee schedule not verified on a primary page.

## Key Question 3: India (and wider South Asia) — WedMeGood, ShaadiSaga, Weddingz, WeddingWire India, Canvera, Urban Company photography, Picxy, creator marketplaces, Sheba.xyz, Pakistan

### Takeaway
In India, the photographer-marketplace ideas that survived are **vendor-subscription lead-gen directories** (WedMeGood, ShaadiSaga → Matrimony.com, Weddingz → OYO); the ones that tried to manage or productize photography (Canvera's photographer platform, Urban Company's photography/wedding category) were written off or pruned. Urban Company explicitly exited photography and wedding services (2019–20) to concentrate on beauty and home, categories with frequency and standardizable SKUs. No Indian or South Asian company was found running an open-bidding photographer marketplace. Sheba.xyz (Bangladesh) survives as a generalist home-services marketplace (Series A $2.9M, 2025); Shadiyana (Pakistan) raised $800k pre-seed (Dec 2025) as a wedding-vendor directory whose revenue driver is venues, not photographers.

### Cited Findings

- Raised Rs 2.7 crore seed from Indian Angel Network (Sept 2015) and $2.6M Series A from Orchid India (April 2019); total ~$3.01M across 2 rounds — [YourStory, 2015](https://yourstory.com/2015/09/wedmegood-funding); [YourStory, 2019](https://yourstory.com/2019/04/online-wedding-platform-wedmegood-funding); [Inc42 company page](https://inc42.com/company/wedmegood/)
- Model: free for couples; vendors pay a quarterly/six-monthly subscription, priority listing costs more; founders said 80% of revenue from subscriptions and 20% from marketing tie-ups (2015–16 interviews) — [Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/lifestyle/wedmegood-making-your-dream-wedding-a-hassle-free-affair/250744); [YourStory, 2016](https://yourstory.com/2016/03/indian-wedding-industry). ## Key Question 4: Reverse-auction / cheapest-bid-wins evidence and marketplace theory relevant to small-market, high-ticket, infrequent services

### Takeaway
Academic work on online labor auctions finds buyers do not simply take the lowest bid, that open (visible) bids outperform sealed bids because they let buyers screen weak bidders, that price dispersion reduces contracting while quality dispersion increases it, and that longer/looser auctions attract lower-quality bidders. Trust-building raises disintermediation risk (Gu & Zhu), and leakage is extreme in some service categories (~90% in one 2018 estimate) but near-zero in others (1 in 83 for home cleaning), i.e., it depends on repeat-interaction structure. Marketplace frameworks (Gurley, Rachitsky, a16z) treat frequency as a core quality factor, and low-frequency/high-value categories force a "wide net" supply strategy and a value-added (managed) take rate.

### Cited Findings

**Reverse auctions and adverse selection**
- Freelance bidding marketplaces are reverse auctions in which sellers compete by offering lower prices; buyers face uncertainty about seller reliability before contracting (adverse selection); Upwork mitigates with ratings and Top Rated programs — [RSM Digital Strategy, 2016](https://digitalstrategy.rsm.nl/2016/10/07/technology-of-the-week-markets-auctions-and-the-gig-economy/)
- Seth Godin on freelancers: "the problem with that race to the bottom is that you might win"; commodity pricing "can't possibly work well for an independent freelancer, because there's always someone cheaper than you" — [Seth's Blog, 2020](https://seths.blog/2020/03/the-problem-for-freelancers/)
- Study of ~1.8M bids on Freelancer.com: open-bid buyer-determined auctions beat sealed-bid on buyer surplus, contract rate and satisfaction, because visible bids let weak bidders self-screen out; sealed-bid auctions get more but "largely unusable" bids — [ERIM / "Open or Sealed Bids in Buyer-Determined Auctions?"](https://www.erim.eur.nl/research/events/detail/3111/); [U. Miami](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/other/How-does-Bid-Visibility-Matter-in/991032069204702976)
- In buyer-determined auctions "the lowest-priced bidder is not the default winner"; sellers may deliver low quality, abscond with advances, hold up or delay jobs — [Job-market paper on freelance platforms](https://rebeccawu96.github.io/RW_Econ_JM/jmp/freelance_jmp_latest.pdf)
- Freelancer.com panel: price dispersion has a consistent negative effect and quality dispersion a positive effect on buyers' contracting — [CORE working paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/301365736.pdf); buyers infer project value from the spread of bids, so more bids = more information — [CORE working paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/301358731.pdf)
- Auctions with longer duration and longer descriptions attract more bids but also more low-quality, less-experienced bidders — [U. Hawaii, "Effect of Auction Design on Bidder Entry"](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/cf4de34e-9ca1-4c97-a782-62c6af40e51f/full)
- Formal treatment: "Nonexclusive competition for a freelancer under adverse selection", Journal of Mathematical Economics — [Bilkent repository](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/2f51901c-8de7-40b4-9b26-d9cdc16298ae/full)

**Disintermediation (taking repeat business off-platform)**
- Gu & Zhu (Management Science, 2021), randomized trial on a freelance marketplace: as a platform improves trust between sides, it faces increased disintermediation risk because both sides circumvent to avoid fees; enhanced trust also increases hiring of high-quality freelancers; leakage detected via chat phrases like "my phone number", "avoid fees", "save 10%", "Venmo" — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/390411); [HBS working paper 18-103](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:hbs:wpaper:18-103)
- Gu (Management Science 2024, Skype-block natural experiment): restricting a hard-to-monitor communication channel cut disintermediation ~18%, more for time-sensitive, communication-intensive, high-skill jobs — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:70:y:2024:i:11:p:7868-7891); [Wharton Mack Institute PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Gu-Grace_Technology-and-Disintermediation-in-Online-Marketplaces.pdf)
- Zhu et al. (2018) estimate ~90% of transactions involve disintermediation after matching in some service platforms; a home-cleaning app study (Astashkina, Bray, Momot, Salikhov) finds no more than 1 in 83 relationships leak — [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-disintermediation); [Kellogg PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/bray/doc/disintermediation/disintermediation.pdf)

**Marketplace frameworks: frequency, liquidity, take rates**
- Bill Gurley, "All Markets Are Not Created Equal" (Nov 2012): among 10 factors, "all things being equal, a higher frequency is obviously better"; others include fragmentation, market size, being in the payment flow, network effects — [repost](https://brandondonnelly.com/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces.md); [Glasp quotes](https://glasp.co/quotes/bill-gurley)
- Rachitsky's survey: 14 of 17 marketplaces went supply-first; Thumbtack was the only one that did not constrain supply, because low-frequency/high-value demand (remodels, plumbing) forced a wide net — [kjlabuz summary](https://kjlabuz.substack.com/p/marketplaceliquidity?s=w); related podcast with Benjamin Lauzier (ex-Lyft, Thumbtack) on liquidity — [Lenny's Podcast listing](https://id.player.fm/series/lennys-podcast-product-growth-career/how-marketplaces-win-liquidity-growth-levers-quality-and-more-benjamin-lauzier-lyft-thumbtack-reforge)
- a16z glossary (Li Jin): managed marketplaces that do more work (logistics, trust, guarantees) can justify higher take rates — [Rosetta summary of a16z](https://rosetta.to/u/a16z/marketplace-breakout-strategies-take-rates-defensibility-payments-field-notes-by); analysis of 25+ marketplaces: more value-added services → higher sustainable take rate; pricing too high drives transactions off-platform ("buyer and seller meet once, trust each other, then cut you out") — [Origami](https://origami-marketplace.com/en-gb/marketplace-take-rate-a-guide-for-marketplace-operators/); [TechCrunch, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/17/4-strategies-for-setting-marketplace-take-rates/)
- Wedding companies have a customer for only ~1 year (engagement to wedding) — [Ypulse, 2019](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2019/03/05/many-tech-startups-have-tried-to-millennialize-the-wedding-industry-but-few-have-ever-succeeded/); vendors on The Knot complain of "tire kickers" (non-serious couples) — [Daily Record, Oct 2026](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/10/15/knot-worldwide-to-acquire-zola-wedding-industry/)
- Economist Al Roth's blog note on the post-engagement wedding market as a market-design problem — [Market Designer blog, Apr 2025](https://marketdesigner.blogspot.com/2025/04/after-proposal-market-for-weddings.html?m=1)

### Inferences
- The empirical literature undermines the premise that clients want "cheapest wins": buyers use bids as information and weigh reputation; price dispersion itself lowers conversion. A platform that forces the lowest bid to win removes the screening buyers actually rely on and should expect lower contract rates, not higher.
- Wedding photography is near the worst case for disintermediation structurally: trust is high-stakes (so platforms must build it), jobs are communication-intensive and time-sensitive (where Gu finds leakage highest), and the photographer–client relationship moves to phone/WhatsApp almost immediately. But the one-time nature of weddings limits the repeat-leakage loss per client; the leak that matters is photographers using the platform as a lead source and closing off-platform on the first job.
- Take-rate theory says a pure matching layer (bids, no fulfilment, no guarantee) can justify only a thin fee, and in a low-ticket market that fee may not cover trust-and-safety costs.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full texts of Gurley's essay or Lenny's Newsletter posts; frequency/ticket-size framing is from excerpts and secondary summaries.
- No study isolates "lowest-bid-selected" as treatment versus quality outcome; the race-to-the-bottom claim rests on theory and practitioner accounts (TaskRabbit, Godin) rather than a clean causal estimate.
- The ~90% Zhu et al. (2018) leakage figure is second-hand.

## Key Question 5: Typical economics — take rates, CAC, repeat rates, seasonality, and what made Snappr/Meero work or not

### Takeaway
Observed take rates cluster at 10–20% for open bidding (Freelancer 10%, Airtasker ~14.5%, Upwork sliding), 15–35% for managed fixed-price (Snappr 15% client + historical 20–35% photographer side; TaskRabbit ~22.5% client-side), and lead fees of $20–90 per inquiry for lead-gen. Wedding demand is one-time (~1-year customer life), lumpy (India's muhurat clusters; Nepal spread but with an April–May peak), and in India vendors fight over leads so hard that a US senator forwarded ~200 fake-lead complaints about the category leader. Snappr's survival came from enterprise repeat volume with zero client fee and negotiated rates; Meero's collapse came after Covid removed its B2B demand base and its photographer pay (€30/hr) left no supply loyalty.

## Key Question 6: Synthesis — recurring lessons on (a) cheapest-bid-wins, (b) disintermediation, (c) quality control and no-shows, (d) small-market liquidity, (e) wedding seasonality; and which transfer to Nepal

### Takeaway
The track record is one-directional: no notable photographer marketplace anywhere has succeeded with open bidding, the best-documented bidding services marketplace (TaskRabbit) abandoned it because completion fell, and every surviving photography platform sets or floors prices and curates supply. The lessons about race-to-the-bottom, leakage and quality transfer to Nepal largely intact (and are amplified by low ticket sizes and WhatsApp-first commerce); the lessons that do not transfer are the enterprise-pivot escape route (thin corporate demand), pay-per-lead pricing (unaffordable at Nepali margins), and the US/EU assumption that a platform can afford to guarantee photographer floors.

### Cited Findings
(a) Cheapest-bid-wins
- TaskRabbit's completion rate fell under bidding despite growth; fixed prices restored utilization and margins and set "an effective floor" avoiding a "race-to-the-bottom" — [TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/17/following-a-drop-in-completed-jobs-errands-marketplace-taskrabbit-shakes-up-its-business-model); [BIA](https://www.bia.com/blog/how-taskrabbit-defied-on-demand-logic-to-reach-breakeven/); [Harvard D^3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/submission/taskrabbit-your-retinue-of-errand-runners)
- Buyers on bidding platforms do not default to the lowest bid and are deterred by price dispersion — [Freelance JMP](https://rebeccawu96.github.io/RW_Econ_JM/jmp/freelance_jmp_latest.pdf); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/301365736.pdf)
- Platforms that positioned as cheapest (Snappr's comparison tool, Kodakit, Meero) drew industry hostility and supply complaints — [Inside Imaging](https://www.insideimaging.com.au/?p=33889); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2019/12/12/kodak-shuttering-kodakit-the-uber-of-photography/); [L'ADN](https://www.ladn.eu/tech-a-suivre/meero-photographe-precarisation/amp/)
- Nepali studios already complain that newcomers undercut and clients "don't understand the criteria of pricing" — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)

(b) Disintermediation
- Trust-building raises leakage; leakage is highest for communication-intensive, time-sensitive, high-skill jobs; a channel block cut it ~18% — [INFORMS Gu & Zhu](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583); [RePEc Gu 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:70:y:2024:i:11:p:7868-7891)
- Leakage ranges from ~90% (some service matching) to 1-in-83 (home cleaning app) — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-disintermediation); [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/bray/doc/disintermediation/disintermediation.pdf)
- Upwork can offer no payment protection or dispute resolution once a contract moves off-platform — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-disintermediation)
- Indian wedding directories sidestep leakage by charging vendors for visibility, not per transaction (WedMeGood 80% subscription revenue) — [Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/lifestyle/wedmegood-making-your-dream-wedding-a-hassle-free-affair/250744)

(c) Quality control and no-shows
- Snappr client reviews recurrently cite no-shows and slow responses (3.8/5, 234 reviews) — [ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/snappr); photographer-side complaints about unclear scope and image quotas — [Inside Imaging](https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/snappr-experience-low-pay-and-unrealistic-expectations/)
- Urban Company narrowed to beauty and home explicitly to "manage and monitor service quality closely", exiting photography/wedding — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/would-be-unicorn-urbanclap-decides-to-operate-in-only-2-service-categories); [DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/urbanclap-non-core-businesses-159072)
- Splento paid photographers £50 bonuses for 10 consecutive 5-star reviews (quality incentive) — [Splento 2018](https://www.splento.com/blog/2018/05/28/splento-photographer-update/); Fotopasal (Nepal) caps itself at 3 events per date to protect quality — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/wedding-photography-nepal-fotopasal.html)
- Lead-gen platforms' quality problem is on the demand side: "tire kickers", alleged fake leads, ~200 complaints to a US senator — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/senator-calls-federal-investigation-wedding-161008184.html); [Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/10/15/knot-worldwide-to-acquire-zola-wedding-industry/)

(d) Liquidity in small/local markets
- Low-frequency, high-value categories force a wide-net supply strategy (Thumbtack the only non-constraining marketplace of 17) — [kjlabuz/Rachitsky](https://kjlabuz.substack.com/p/marketplaceliquidity?s=w); frequency is a core marketplace quality factor — [Gurley repost](https://brandondonnelly.com/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces.md)
- Snappr publishes one price per country, not per city, and 484 cities on fixed prices (liquidity by standardization rather than local price discovery) — [Snappr pricing](https://www.snappr.com/photography-pricing)
- Managed marketplaces earn higher take rates only by adding services (trust, guarantees, logistics) — [Rosetta/a16z](https://rosetta.to/u/a16z/marketplace-breakout-strategies-take-rates-defensibility-payments-field-notes-by); [Origami](https://origami-marketplace.com/en-gb/marketplace-take-rate-a-guide-for-marketplace-operators/)
- Shadiyana (Pakistan) earns primarily from venues even though it lists photographers — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1998854)

(e) Seasonality of wedding demand
- India: muhurat clustering, year-ahead bookings, Kharmas pause, growing non-muhurat share (34%→57% of Nov–Dec venue bookings, 2019→2022) — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/2364148-muhurats-are-a-thing-of-yesteryear---couples-embrace-non-mahurat-dates---find-out-why); [HappyFares](https://happyfares.in/blog/?p=12437)
- Nepal: year-round with April–May peak; photographers describe work as "sustainable but seasonal" — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2019/04/16/event-planners-get-busy-as-wedding-season-starts); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-wedding-photographer-challenges.html)
- Weddings yield a ~1-year customer; companies that survived (Meero→Diffusely, Snappr enterprise) moved to repeat B2B demand — [Ypulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2019/03/05/many-tech-startups-have-tried-to-millennialize-the-wedding-industry-but-few-have-ever-succeeded/); [FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/diffusely-ex-meero-rebondit-sur-son-pivot-et-leve-11-millions-deuros/457692); [Snappr enterprise](https://www.snappr.com/enterprise-editing)

### Inferences
Lessons that transfer to Nepal (low-income, small, WhatsApp-first market):
- Cheapest-wins will select for the newcomers incumbents already complain about and will repel the quality studios (Fotopasal-type) whose presence a wedding client most values; TaskRabbit's completion-rate collapse is the expected failure mode, and it will be worse because a wedding cannot be re-done.
- Leakage will be near-total on first contact: wedding shoots are exactly the communication-intensive, time-sensitive jobs where Gu finds the most circumvention, and Nepali commerce already runs on phone/WhatsApp/eSewa; a transaction take rate therefore cannot be the primary revenue line. Vendor-subscription (WedMeGood/Shadibox) or deposit-escrow-with-a-reason (date-lock guarantee, dispute cover) are the models that survive leakage.
- Quality/no-show risk must be handled by curation and caps (Splento bonuses, Fotopasal's 3-event cap, Urban Company's "manage quality closely") rather than ratings alone; a bid-only platform has no lever on this.
- Small-market liquidity: Kathmandu Valley plus Pokhara may carry one city-level marketplace; Snappr's answer (one national fixed price, many cities) and Shadiyana's (monetize venues, list photographers) are more realistic than per-job price discovery.
- Seasonality: Nepal's spread-out calendar is actually friendlier than India's muhurat spikes, but a one-time customer base still means CAC is paid every year; corporate/event/real-estate/product photography (the demand Snappr, Meero, Splento survived on) is the counter-seasonal filler to design for from day one.

Lessons that do not transfer:
- The enterprise/Fortune-500 escape route (Snappr) and fashion/auto SaaS pivot (Diffusely) require a deep corporate content market Nepal lacks at scale.
- Pay-per-lead pricing at Thumbtack/Bark levels ($20–90) is impossible against Nepali ticket sizes; even India's directories use flat subscriptions instead.
- Guaranteed photographer floors (Shoott $100/hr) presuppose consumer willingness to pay $25/photo; Nepal would need a floor denominated in local wedding norms and likely cannot fund it from platform margin.
- US-style BBB/senatorial scrutiny of fake leads is absent, which lowers compliance cost but also means trust must be earned by the platform brand itself, as WedMeGood's 2017 anti-impersonation notice shows is already a live issue in South Asia.

### Gaps
- No example anywhere of a photographer marketplace that ran cheapest-bid-wins long enough to generate outcome data; the negative case is built from adjacent services (TaskRabbit, Freelancer.com) plus the universal choice of fixed/managed pricing by photography platforms.
- No Nepal-specific data on wedding photography price bands, number of active photographers, or how bookings are currently sourced (word of mouth vs. Facebook/Instagram), which would be needed to size liquidity.
- Urban Company's internal rationale for dropping photography (unit economics vs. standardization vs. frequency) was not retrievable in full text.
